## Purpose

Part IV (*The Policy Surface*) gives the **primitives** --- policy, redline, acute/chronic,
the read channel, second-order free energy. This part gives the **measurement devices**:
the abstract experimental design that would settle Part III's bets by reading, off a
compiled body of coupled policies, *where epistemic surprise enters and how it propagates.*

This is methodology, not application. Everything here describes a **compiled policy graph**
generically; the named substrate, the product, and any real dataset are out of scope by
construction. Where this part anchors to an external standard --- NASA-STD-7009A --- it names it
directly and among peers (Sandia's **PCMM**, **ASME V&V 40**), because the *method* of using
a credibility scale as an entry-prior is general and carries no instantiation.

One claim organizes the whole construction, and it is a refusal:

> **Epistemic certainty is not a number. It is a family of perspectival maps.**

A single calibrated risk index is not a conservative summary of that family --- it is a
*different and worse object*, one that erases the frame-dependence that makes the same
residual aleatory to one subsystem and epistemic to another. The devices below are built so
that their deliverable stays **topological and typed**, never a scalar. The near-term,
buildable payoff (Section 6) is narrower still: not the full map, but *which relations are at risk
of super-critical surprise propagation* --- a result that is falsifiable today, before any
calibration exists.

---

## 1. Epistemic certainty is frame-relative

A loop resolves only its **margin** --- the few axes of `s` its frame spans (Part IV Section 2.2). An entity it couples to has bulk in dimensions orthogonal to that frame; that bulk arrives as *apparent noise*. Widen the frame, and the noise resolves into structure. So the classical split is frame-relative:

- **Aleatory** uncertainty = the irreducible-*for-you* residual, from either of two sources:   bulk orthogonal to your frame (your `s` cannot represent it --- widen the frame and *this*   resolves into structure) or genuine stochasticity on an axis you already resolve (the dead noise floor --- no framing reduces it).
- **Epistemic** uncertainty = your margin --- the part you could, in principle, learn.

The same residual is therefore aleatory to subsystem A and epistemic to subsystem B, with no
contradiction. Probabilistic risk assessment (PRA) collapses every frame into a magnitude, never asking *which epistemic perspective* each marginalized aleatory residual sits on. But marginalizing residual aleatory uncertainty between two viewpoints, as they are reporting from different frames and there's no universal transfer function between arbitrary perspectives.

Instead of one consolidated number, the proper measurement device is a map. The model's interconnectivity, with each residual's aleatory and epistemic uncertainty classified by the class of that dynamic according to Part II's four floors:

| floor | meaning | aleatory / epistemic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **through → zero** | mastered; model transparent | reducible (epistemic, resolved) |
| **living floor** (structured, learnable, never zero) | a live sub-model still moving | irreducible-but-modelable |
| **dead noise floor** (shapeless, stationary) | nothing to learn | pure aleatory |
| **necrosis / phantom** (feedback cut, confabulates) | a stale assumption trusted as live | the dangerous case |

PRA reports a magnitude and never types it. **Necrosis is the executive-terrifying floor** ---
a critical interface assumed nominal because no redline has arrived lately (Part IV Section 5.4). The deliverable of this part is the typed family, never the magnitude. These are the network-identification questions Part II's Bet 2 names: *construct the shared target each
frame can model*, and *let each frame read its own subgraph and margins of it.*

## 2. The compiled target is a modeled egregore, not a head

To measure a network you must compile its frames into **one** common linked dataset --- the
**substrate**. That is correct and necessary; *naming* the substrate is precisely what lets
each frame project onto it. The danger was never compiling. It is **how the artifact is
used**, and the same compiled graph is the trap or the goal depending on the direction
surprise flows across it:

- Used as a **head** --- a single authoritative number descends, and the sub-disciplines steer
  open-loop between its verdicts. This is *seeing like a state* (Part II, *Head, field, or
  feeling*). The trap.
- Used as a **modeled egregore** --- each frame reads the compiled whole from its own margin,
  computes its *own* second-order surprise against it (Part IV Section 6.1), and adjusts both its
  internal design and its point-to-point edges. Verdicts never descend; the center is a
  *referent each periphery reads.* The goal.

The materialist refinement is load-bearing, not decoration. **An egregore is always an as-if
stance, never an entity.** The compiled dataset is the real, material substrate; the egregore
is *each frame's as-if projection onto it*; the head failure is treating one projection as the
substrate's verdict. The ladder *mechanism → egregore → sovereign* is not degrees of realness
but **reciprocity of the as-if** (no projection → one-way projection → mutual modeling).

The consequence has teeth: **accountability that lands only on a faceless egregore cannot be
felt or discharged** --- only the real substrates it is projected over (the member loops, which
really suffer) can receive and act on it; a system in which accountability evaporates at the
projection level is measurably failure-prone. A head therefore loses the closed-loop signal
that would keep the target a living referent: each verdict descending from a single node sheds
the distributed read-channels that catch its own drift, producing the necrosis pattern (Section 5) ---
locally fine nodes sharing a stale model. That is the instrumental reason the compiled target
must stay a referent and never a head. The head / field / feeling discriminator from Part III
is the guardrail that keeps it one.

## 3. The compression / correction gap

The egregore and the propagation simulation are two computations of different cost, and the
map lives between them:

> The **egregore** is the cheap *perspectival* compression each frame animates. The
> **propagation simulation** is the expensive *non-perspectival* correction computed on the
> substrate. The epistemic-certainty map is the **gap** between them.

Surprise propagation is exactly the non-local field effect a *local* as-if projection cannot
predict --- a frame, animating its cheap compression, cannot see where a perturbation three
hops away rings back into its margin. The simulation is the bounded view-from-nowhere that
catches where the cheap projection is wrong. So the compiled substrate is *both* the referent
each frame animates *and* the thing you simulate to correct those animations.

The correction does not close. The simulation is **itself lossy** --- it runs on the legible,
compiled graph, and the worst couplings are illegible (Section 6, Section 8). So the correction *shrinks*
the egregore-residual but never drives it to zero. The honest map therefore carries a
**standing residual term**: the part of the non-local field no simulation captures. There are
two levels of it, both on the map --- the **carved-off forms** (the bestiary of Part III, named
and modeled) and the **irreducible remainder after all of them** (the standing floor).

> **Driving the residual to zero is the phantom error** --- confabulated completeness, the
> seeing-like-a-state failure at field scale. A truthful map keeps a *here-be-dragons* floor,
> and **its presence, not its absence, is the sign the map is honest.**

## 4. The hard constraint: topological and typed, not metric

Part IV Section 4 is explicit: there is no universal magnitude metric, and *a shipped threshold is a
preference masquerading as a fact.* This constrains every device below.

The temptation is a Monte-Carlo simulation that rolls up to one calibrated risk number. That
**rebuilds the head** --- it manufactures the single descending magnitude Section 2 forbids. The
discipline:

- Use simulation for **reachability and propagation only** --- perturb a node, walk downstream,
  count which edges fire (acute vs chronic, Part IV Section 6.6), measure the ring-out radius.
- The deliverable is a **typed propagation map**: which regions conduct, which dampen, which
  floor each residual settles to. Never a risk index.

A typed map can be *acted on* (decouple here, thicken there) without ever pretending to a
total order over harms. That is the whole point.

## 5. The propagation model

**Substrate.** A compiled policy graph: nodes are policies `{o, s, C, p(o∣s)}` (Part IV Section 2),
directed edges are typed relations. Standard graph operations suffice --- extract the subgraph
seeded from a node, expand it along downstream reachability, take unions and differences over
policy sets. No specialized machinery is assumed.

**A redline conditions on its entry channel.** The same node throws differently depending on
which relation a surprise arrived through --- surprise is *about the relationship* (your model
of the other broke), so it enters **edges**, not only nodes (Part IV Section 3). The rigorous state
space for propagation is therefore the **line graph** ---
states are `(node, entry-relation)` pairs, not bare nodes. The node-level reduction below is
an approximation, sufficient for the MVP's ordinal ranking; the line-graph form is the
refinement for the full build.

**Two per-element quantities, both ordinal at first:**

- **Edge conductance** `c(u→v) ≥ 0` --- how much surprise the relation conducts. Set by the
  **sign-gate** and the **thickness profile** of the edge (Part III's bestiary: a co-pointed,
  negative-envelope edge dampens; an imposed, positive-meet edge conducts and can ring).
  `c = 0` is a connection that exists but does not conduct.

**Sign as marginal computation.** Conductance `c(u→v)` materializes the *sign* of this relation as a marginal calculation: how strongly the other’s normative motion moves your `C` in this shared context. High `c` means their policy gradient has a strong effect on your outcomes (the relation is normatively live); `c = 0` means your `C`s are orthogonal here --- normatively numb in this context even if the relation exists. Conductance is context-dependent: the same pair can have high `c` on one referent and `c = 0` on another (adversaries in trade, coordinators on a shared safety standard). Absorption `a(v)` is the normative wound-buffer: a node with high absorption acknowledges a significant policy hit and integrates it without emitting it onward --- thick tolerance, wounds metabolized rather than passed on.

- **Node absorption** `a(v) > 0` --- the per-node tolerance (thickness) that absorbs incoming
  surprise before it is re-emitted onward.

**The avalanche model.** Propagation is a threshold cascade over dense nonlinear
coupling. **Criticality is coupling relative to per-node absorption --- not density alone.**
Define a weighted **transmission matrix** `T` over the (line-graph) states, with the
node-level approximation

```
T[v→w]  ≈  c(v→w) / a(v)
```

--- the expected onward redline on edge `v→w` per redline `v` receives. The **branching ratio**
of a region `S` is the spectral radius `ρ(T_S)` of `T` restricted to `S`. This is the basic
reproduction number `R₀`, localized:

> **ρ(T_S) > 1 ⇒ a super-critical "avalanche core":** a small surprise entering `S` grows
> rather than decays.

**Scope limitation --- passive conductance only.** `T ≥ 0` (since `c ≥ 0`, `a > 0`) means every
entry encodes *passive* conductance: an edge either transmits surprise forward or it does not.
Dampening in this model is the absence of transmission (`c → 0`), never active cancellation.
A node whose coupling is actively suppressed by a regulator --- the annealing mechanism
hypothesized below and the SOC self-tuning toward ρ ≈ 1 are both instances of exactly this
kind of active counter-steering --- is indistinguishable in `T` from a node that is simply
quiet. Both appear low-ρ. The controlled-but-fragile case --- a sub-network held at low apparent ρ by an active regulator
that is itself one broken edge from failing --- cannot be seen in `T`, and is one structural
origin of the Section 6 cold-region dragon. Two patches, for two cold subtypes `T` cannot tell
apart:

- **Necrotic-cold** (low ρ because the edge is dead or unmodeled) is caught by 7009A --- it is
  exactly the *low ρ + low 7009A* cell of Section 8.
- **Actively-regulated-cold** (low ρ because a live regulator is counter-steering) can carry
  *high* 7009A: the models are validated; the control loop's robustness is not. It hides in
  Section 8's *low ρ + high 7009A* ("healthy; monitor") cell, where the validation scale cannot
  see it. Its patch is the **Section 7 large-perturbation probe** --- a regulator one edge from
  failing is a subcritical bifurcation, linearly stable but large-kick vulnerable --- not 7009A.

These two subtypes, plus passive attunement, are separable *without* signed `T`, from the redline
log itself. An active regulator continuously consumes surprise and emits corrective redlines
internally: high internal churn, low *net* out-propagation. Passive attunement is genuinely quiet:
low churn. Necrosis is dark: no traffic, stale head (Part IV Section 3.3, liveness).

| cold subtype | net ρ | internal redline traffic | read channel / head |
|---|---|---|---|
| passive attunement | low | low | advancing |
| active regulation (fragile) | low | **high** | advancing |
| necrosis | low | none | **stale** |

Throughput thus decomposes the absorption term `a(v)` --- which otherwise lumps "high threshold,
silent" together with "low threshold, high counter-emission" --- into its passive and active
halves: the dynamic discriminator the static spectrum lacks. The signed-`T` v2 is the *structural*
route to the same distinction (separating passive-quiet from actively-regulated-quiet at the cost
of the clean Perron--Frobenius threshold --- the spectral story becomes complex-plane eigenvalue
distributions governing oscillation and metastability rather than monotone growth), but throughput
is the cheaper first line, available from data the protocol already produces (Part IV Section 6.4).

**Self-organized criticality is an open question, not an assumption.** Teams may self-tune to
the edge of breakage (adding coupling until something rings). If they do, avalanche sizes are
**power-law** --- the tail risk is irreducible and the map can only measure *susceptibility*,
not *predict* events. Do not assume it. **Test it:** the avalanche-size distribution is
power-law (critical) versus exponential (sub-critical). 
**Optimal propagation regime --- pragmatic vs. epistemic fit.** Cascade risk has three
regimes that correspond to distinct failure modes, and the target is not simply *low*
cascade risk:

- **Sub-critical (ρ << 1) --- too dampened.** Surprise from a changed source node is
  absorbed locally. The source achieves *pragmatic fit* (it updates its own behavior)
  but the ring-out is too weak to update neighbors. The rest of the network retains
  stale models of the source and coordinates as if nothing changed --- distributed
  necrosis: each node appears locally fine, but the network’s shared model of the
  environment is wrong.
- **Super-critical (ρ >> 1) --- too much ring.** Surprise amplifies and circulates.
  The network never settles to a new equilibrium; redlines keep propagating. Chronic
  thrash --- neither pragmatic nor epistemic fit, because the network cannot converge
  (Part IV Section 6.6: a chronic redline means the policy itself is wrong, but the sender
  cannot integrate the lesson).
- **Critical (ρ ≈ 1) --- the productive regime.** Surprise propagates far enough to
  update the models of relevant neighbors (*epistemic fit*), then decays. Acute rather
  than chronic. The health signature from Part IV: redline bursts followed by quiet
  attunement.

The target is *controlled propagation*: enough ring for the network to achieve shared
epistemic fit when a source node’s environment changes; not so much that it thrashes.
This also reframes the SOC question above: if teams self-tune to ρ ≈ 1, that may be
adaptive rather than pathological --- optimal information transmission at the edge of
cascades is a known signature of healthy complex systems.

**Temperature spike as network-level wound.** A sharp increase in `dΔ/dt` --- the rate at which the collective deviation from the shared `C` grows --- is the network-level analog of a normative wound. Like a wound to a sovereign (`C` violated → free energy spike), it is normatively significant (the collective moved away from its preferred states), it drives a response (the annealing mechanism activates), and it is traceable to specific node-changes. The difference from a sovereign wound: it is distributed --- no single node experiences it entirely, but the gradient is real and measurable. *“No wound lands on a single someone”* is a statement about accountability topology, not about whether the wound exists. Chronic thrash (ρ >> 1) is therefore the network-level analog of a wound that cannot heal: the annealing temperature stays elevated, the collective deviation does not converge, and no epistemic fit is achieved.

The answer changes what the map
claims.

## 6. The MVP --- localize the super-critical sub-networks (build this first)

The full epistemic-certainty map is a long build (Section 8). One modest deliverable pays handsome
dividends and **stands alone**: identify the relations and clusters at high risk of
super-critical propagation. **Criticality is sub-network-specific** --- a body holds
avalanche-prone pockets inside an overall sub-critical matrix --- so the deliverable needs *no*
global verdict, *no* calibrated entry distribution, and *no* full map.

**The computable handle.** Per region, the intuitive form is

```
R  ≈  (edge coupling × out-degree) / (per-node absorption)
```

and the precise form is `ρ(T_S) > 1` (Section 5) --- computable on the compiled graph by seeded
subgraph extraction plus the edge weights. `R > 1` marks an avalanche core.

**Predict vulnerability, not the cascade result.** We do not model what an avalanche *does*
(size, path, damage) --- only *which portions of the graph are vulnerable to one.* Vulnerability
is a **linear-stability** property --- *where does a small surprise grow?* --- and that is exactly
what the leading eigenvalue gives. So the tool's linearity is **sufficient, not a
limitation**; the nonlinear outcome is out of scope *by design*. Two consequences:

1. The MVP is a **deterministic spectral computation** --- eigenvalues and eigenvectors of `T`
   --- *not* a Monte-Carlo. (A cascade simulator demotes to an optional *outcome*-explorer for
   later, and stays subject to Section 4.)
2. Validation simplifies to a **binary clustering check** --- no size-matching, no calibration.

**Why this is the right MVP --- it dodges the hard gates:**

- **Ordinal-only.** It needs *ranked* edge coupling and node absorption --- a **reduced
  two-parameter bestiary** --- not a calibrated `p(o∣s)`. It therefore skips the estimator gap
  (Section 8) and skips the worry that the full bestiary proliferates (Part III, probe gate Q-c).
- **Conditional.** It claims *if* surprise enters here, it avalanches --- independent of *where*
  surprise actually enters. That makes it robust to the calibration gap entirely.
- **Validatable now, pre-calibration.** Test the predicted cores against **historical
  change-cascades** mined from version history (logical / change-coupling: sets of nodes that
  historically moved together). The question is binary --- *did past cascades concentrate in the
  predicted-vulnerable regions, more than chance?* It needs neither 7009A nor a calibrated
  model, and it can prove the method wrong.
- **Executive-legible and actionable.** A ranked list of avalanche-risk interfaces says:
  *decouple, thicken, or buffer here.* It makes the otherwise-abstract structural-uncertainty
  result concrete --- *architectural tail-risk, fixable only by decoupling, not by more
  analysis.*

**Three refinements (do not oversell the heat-map):**

- **Escape, not just presence.** Highest risk is a super-critical core **with high-conductance
  bridges to the rest.** An isolated pocket (thin boundary) is contained; a bridged one
  *exports* avalanches. Rank by `core-criticality × boundary-escape`, where boundary-escape
  weights the conductance of edges leaving `S` by the dragon eigenvector's mass near them. The
  action then includes *insulate the boundary* (the firewall), not only *decouple the core.*
- **Dynamic.** Cores shift; one new tight coupling can flip a pocket super-critical. Re-run per
  release, and re-anchor as each release generates samples (Part IV Section 3.3, liveness).
- **Covers only the mapped.** Illegible and tacit couplings --- 7009A's blind spot, the necrotic
  nodes (Section 8) --- can carry the worst avalanche and are **invisible here by construction.** Wear
  the residual (Section 3). The real danger is the **false security of the cold regions**, where in a
  truly critical system the power-law tail lives.

This reframes the near-term program: not *build the full epistemic-certainty map* but *find
the avalanche-risk relations.* The full map can grow from this; the payoff stands alone.

**From vulnerability map to control prescription.** The same eigenstructure that *locates* an
avalanche core also says which edge to move, and which way. First-order eigenvalue perturbation
gives the sensitivity of the branching ratio to any single weight:

    ∂ρ(T_S) / ∂T[v→w]  =  y_v · x_w / (y · x)

where `x` is the leading right eigenvector of `T_S` (the **dragon** of §7 --- where surprise
pools) and `y` its leading left eigenvector (the **co-dragon** --- where surprise enters most
effectively). Edges with the largest `y_v · x_w` move `ρ` most per unit change; they are the
control surface. The §6 "escape" weighting already reads half of this (the right-eigenvector mass
`x`); the left eigenvector `y` is the other half, and the product ranks the actionable edges.

So the measurement hands over a knob, not only a warning:

- **Super-critical core (ρ >> 1, chronic thrash).** Pull it toward `ρ ≈ 1` by *thinning* its
  highest-sensitivity edges (lower `c`) or *thickening* absorption at high-`y` nodes (raise `a`).
- **Sub-critical pocket (ρ << 1, distributed necrosis --- stale models, no ring-out).** Lift it
  toward `ρ ≈ 1` by the reverse: raise conductance on the high-sensitivity edges, or lower an
  over-thick absorption.

Three disciplines keep this honest. (i) It stays **topological, not metric** (§4): the output is
*thicken here, thin there*, an ordinal ranking of edges, never a target value like "set ρ = 0.97."
The objective is per-core **controlled propagation** (§5's critical regime), and criticality is
sub-network-specific (§6) --- there is no global dial. (ii) It is a **local, first-order**
prescription around the current operating point; the eigenvectors shift as you act, so it is a
gradient to iterate per release (§6, *Dynamic*), not a one-shot solve. (iii) **A drop in `ρ` is
not automatically a win.** Lowering apparent `ρ` by raising absorption can be genuine passive
thickening *or* can manufacture a cold-region dragon by burying active-regulation fragility (§5,
*Scope limitation*). Verify any control-induced quieting with redline **throughput**, not the new
`ρ` alone: passive quiet shows low churn, actively-regulated quiet shows high internal churn at
low net out-propagation. The governance acts that *deploy* these edits belong to Part VII (§7);
the measurement supplies only the ranked knob and its sensitivity.

## 7. Two spectral objects, and the hand-off to Part VII

The cascade analysis is **spectral**, which makes it a close relative of PCA. Two spectral
objects fall out of the same computation:

- **Top of the transmission matrix `T`** (the leading eigenvector) = the **dominant
  propagation mode** --- where surprise spreads furthest. The *dragon.* (This is `R` localized.)
- **Bottom of the graph Laplacian `L`** (the Fiedler vector and low modes) = the **natural
  seams** --- candidate ownership boundaries.

An **orphaned propagation vector** is a dominant `T`-mode whose support is **spread across the
current ownership partition** (Conway's law) --- a cascade no single team's blanket contains, so
no one predicts or owns it.

The *measurement* surfaces these. The *governance acts* they enable belong to **Part VII**,
and this part only remarks their utility:

- **Contain by re-partition** --- align the seams (`L`) to the dragons (`T`) so each dominant
  mode lands *inside one owner*, who then internalizes the cascade as a normatively predictable concern rather
  than an orphaned cross-boundary one. A Conway's-law re-org informed **analytically**, not
  politically: a governance act driven by a measurement.
- **Steward the rest** --- no partition orphans nothing (containing one mode splits another: the
  frustrated-network no-free-lunch), and the illegible residual cannot be spectrally found at
  all. So assign a **dragon-owner**, a sovereign whose explore-loop is to relate to and map the
  orphaned vector --- the residual given a keeper rather than a solution (Part III; the residual
  is never vanquished, only diminished).

**Metastability caveat --- the one nonlinear residue.** Regions that are linearly stable (small
surprises decay) can still be vulnerable to a *large* kick --- a subcritical bifurcation.
Eigen-analysis marks them *cold/safe*, but they are exactly the **cold-region dragons** where the power-law tail lives. The **actively-regulated-cold** sub-network of Section 5 --- held quiet by a live regulator one edge from failing --- is the same animal from the propagation side: 7009A validates its models but not its control loop, so this probe, not the validation scale, is its test. They join the steward bucket, or get chased by a second-pass large-perturbation Monte-Carlo. Cold stays un-trusted *by policy*, not by accident. And the re-partition is an **optimization** (contain the worst, steward the rest), never a clean solve.

## 8. Calibration --- the longer build (the estimator gap)

The MVP needs only ordinal ranks. The full epistemic-certainty map needs an **estimator** for
`p(o∣s)` and a policy's valid region (its flight envelope) --- and Part IV gives the propagation
algebra but no estimator. This is the larger half of the build.

**The entry prior (which nodes are epistemically under-anchored --- *where* surprise enters).**
Rather than invent a calibrated estimator, anchor on **NASA-STD-7009A**'s Credibility
Assessment Scale --- an ordinal rating across factors such as Validation, Input Pedigree,
Results Uncertainty/Robustness, Use History, V&V, Management, and People (verify the exact
factor set against the standard). Low Validation / Input-Pedigree marks an under-anchored node
--- a likely entry point and avalanche initiator. The rating is **ordinal**, not a `p(o∣s)`
shape, which is exactly why it fits the topological-not-metric constraint (Section 4). The scale is
the *cheap perspectival prior*; **observed surprise over releases is the correction** ---
re-anchor as each release generates samples. Cite the scale among peers (Sandia
**PCMM**, **ASME V&V 40**) as good practice --- it shows the survey and avoids a domain tell.

**The residual hard part --- and it is exactly the dangerous one.** 7009A rates *formal*
modeling and simulation. The tacit, un-formalized policies --- the necrotic and phantom nodes
(Part IV Section 5.4) --- resist the scale: they have no V&V record to grade. **7009A is blind precisely
where the worst surprises hide.** The complement is the *hidden-axis* hook: an apparent
like-for-like coupling that conceals an unmodeled dimension (Part III). The honest map carries
this as part of its standing residual (Section 3), 
**7009A source nodes as natural experiments.** The most formally validated nodes ---
those with active 7009A records --- are natural probes into cascade dynamics. When the
environment changes and a source node’s `p(o∣s)` is invalidated, the resulting surprise
injection is real (not synthetic), traceable (the validation history records what broke
and when), and scoped to a specific entry point. Compare actual propagation from these
natural experiments to the spectral prediction from Sections 5--6:

- Does surprise reach N-hop neighbors as predicted by ρ?
- Does the cascade settle (acute) or persist (chronic)?
- Which neighbors emit redlines, and which absorb silently?

This grounds the spectral map in observable events and makes it testable before any
calibrated entry-prior exists. It also supplies a direct measure of *how much the
network resonates to a change in source-node environment* --- a key indicator of whether
the system is in the sub-critical (learning stays local), critical (productive
propagation), or super-critical (chronic thrash) regime described in Section 5.

**Cascade risk and epistemic overfitting.** Cascade risk is not equivalent to epistemic
overfittedness, but overfitting is its most dangerous source. High ρ can arise from two
distinct causes:

1. **Genuinely tight physical coupling** --- models are correct; the architecture is risky
   but well-understood. Response: decouple or insulate the architecture.
2. **Models calibrated too narrowly on historical patterns** --- low absorption because
   `p(o∣s)` was fit to a specific operating regime and generalizes poorly. Response:
   re-calibrate, expand the model’s valid region.

The two signals together give the diagnostic:

| | Low 7009A (under-validated) | High 7009A (well-validated) |
|---|---|---|
| **High ρ** | Likely overfitting --- narrow models, brittle; re-calibrate | Genuine tight coupling; decouple architecture |
| **Low ρ** | Stale / necrotic --- latent risk not yet visible; probe liveness | Healthy; monitor |

The dangerous cell is *low ρ + low 7009A*: a necrotic edge reads as quiet and therefore
safe, until the latent coupling surfaces and the ring-out is catastrophic. Cold regions
stay untrusted by policy, not by accident (Section 6 caveat).

not as a gap to be papered over.

**Q-gates --- conditions on Bet 2's kernel**

Q-a, Q-c, Q-g gate Bet 2 directly: the co-held policy profile cannot be used as a transmission kernel in Bet 2 or Section 5 until each has a defensible answer. **Q-d is the estimator-level basis for Bet 2** --- the dyadic claim that, with a matched lossy dynamics model held fixed, adding a learned normative model improves prediction; if it fails, profile cannot predict propagation at the network level. It underpins Bet 2 rather than blocking it. Q-b, Q-e, Q-f are further open empirical bets the framework makes that do not block execution.
