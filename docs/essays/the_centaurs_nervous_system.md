# Part I: The Centaur's Nervous System

Imagine learning how to wield a sword. When you start, there is a sword, and there is you. They are separate and you feel them as separate. At this stage you are nearly as likely to injure yourself as you are to strike your target. Now imagine training with this instrument for years. You start to know the position of the sword without having to see it. You can feel through the sword and intimately know the force a particular strike will impart. Are you and the sword separate? You are separable, but with training that instrument becomes an extension of your self-hood in a manner totally different from the experience the beginner faces. Both your internal world as well as your physical musculature and nervous system have adapted and adopted this extension. You are materially different compared to who you were before you started training.

One step further. Imagine you are a knight in training. A knight is dangerous not so much
because of their sword, but because they are a mounted warrior. The horse and the man unite to
become a much more dangerous entity than either on their own. But once again we are faced
with the same progression. When you begin, your steed is separate from yourself. You are
just as likely to injure yourself at this stage as to move and operate according to your intentions.
You must train a long time before horse and identity are united.

The master horseback rider and the master swordsman both have changed their physical and mental identities. But the means and matter of their respective transformations are very different. For a horseback rider to be effective, both the horse and the rider must develop mutual knowledge and trust. For the swordsman to be effective they must know the nature of their blade. Whatever possible awareness within the blade is moot. The action of the wielder solely determines the blade's position. Whereas the horse and rider have an ongoing negotiation on who is in charge, whose will leads and whose will follows.

What distinguishes both these masters from beginners is how their felt sense extends through and beyond their body into their instrument. They have subconscious and pre-analytical feedback from the sword, from the horse. Relative to their motives, they can feel what movement is "right" or "wrong".

The OODA loop, coined by Colonel John Boyd points out how some entities, like humans and horses, observe, orient, decide, and act (OODA) in an ongoing dynamic loop. Some entities, like swords and hammers, do not. But critically, living beings are composed of a countless number of such loops. Each cell has numerous OODA loops, each organ, perhaps each brain hemisphere its own. Yet within that cacophony is you. Multiple OODA loops do not define what is or isn't you, instead how they interact---who is "inside" the other's loop determines where the locus of action lives.

But inside doesn't mean what you think it means. A cell is physically inside me, but my cellular OODA loops are not 'inside' my phenomenology. What determines who is inside versus who is outside is not a question of frequency or material size. What is inside vs outside is a question of whose principles are most respected. Boyd didn't realize it to my knowledge but what it means to be inside means to be _normatively inside_ another loop. Let me explain.

Each loop is inherently faced with many possible decisions. The 'decide' component tells us that within the loop there's the possibility space of more than one possible action, and an ordering of those possibilities that culminates into action. How they decide is an internal preference that constitutes a big piece of their identity. Yet _how_ they decide is different than _what_ they decide. What's decided is a ranking of possible next states based on what's immediately available. This is how one loop can be 'inside' the other; By exerting influence over what another 'sees' as available.

Influencing what another loop decides is not the same as deciding _for_ the loop, because inherent in the concept of observing another loop is it is other -- its processes are external to yours. So being normatively inside another is to influence it by changing what it pays attention to, not to consume it. By changing what it pays attention to, the 'normatively inside' loop recursively influences what the other loop observes, where it orients, and therefore what it decides and enacts. It is entirely feasible for a smaller, slower entity to be inside the other's OODA loop if they can change what another loop pays attention to. When one entity can feel into the other's loop, correctly anticipating when why and how their OODA will function, they can extend Tai Chi's effortless effort---the directing of external force according to their principles.

Note that we have a built in understanding that feeling is normative. We all have first hand experience of how much of our inner lives are value laden: this tastes good/bad; that looks beautiful or abhorrent, etc. But we can start to answer why. Feeling is the negotiation between how we think something we're connected to _should_ act versus how we observe it acting.

This is what determines the difference between a beginning rider and experienced cavalry. Going into battle is fearful. A skillful rider must accept that fear and acknowledge it. They must predict its limits and console the fear that two warm-blooded animals face in an existential situation. Whether you charge or balk is a question of who you are. Are you the horse? Yes. Are you the rider? Yes. When you accept you are both and neither then you can orient both towards what you value instead of what scares you.

The rider points to another important fact related to multi OODA loop mastery. Control can have loci, but where such loci are situated is scale dependent in a way that optimizes local processing speed. This is what separates effective organizations (and organisms) from ineffective ones. The rider is "within" the horse's OODA loop in a manner that is effectively different than how the horse internalizes the rider. In a multi OODA loop body, each OODA loop must develop "right feel" for the others. The rider feels the horse, the horse feels the rider. For them to be martially effective, each must develop right feel and _right_ depends on subjective perspective.

What does the master drone operator embody? Do the autonomous swarms form an extension of her will, or is her unique agency diluted and buffeted without firm control of whose OODA loop is inside whose. Is it the company that made the AI targeting software inside her loop or is she inside theirs? What role do the chips and hardware which constitute each drone play? Is it the general whose command told the operator to send the attack force? Perhaps the drones themselves are in control? The answer to this question is an OODA loop question. Who is most inside whom? And the answer to that question is best couched as one of proprioception. Whoever has a firmer 'felt sense' of the other can use that sense to effectively extend effortless effort to direct their energy. When there is no such felt sense, or the sense of feeling is spread uniformly through the OODA actors, an egregore appears. Such spirits show themselves when control is so diluted that the body has no functioning head, no entity to claim responsibility for the principles enacted.

War and martial arts are useful for describing these phenomena because they paint action and intention in such stark outlines. In essence, they are a cartoon version of reality. There is us, there is them, there is our power, and there is theirs. But please note how this story transcends warfare and extends throughout our ancient and modern lives. The sword can just as easily be a pen; The horse can just as easily be a pet; the drone an autonomous car. What's important is the meditation on what it means to be me, given that I can change who I am purely based on what I can effectively feel.

But what does right feel consist of? I cannot pretend to offer a complete answer this question, but we can observe some properties by breaking down 'right' and 'feel'.

Feeling is multi-directional. Unlike mechanistic descriptions of cause and effect feeling works for sensation and for movement. Feeling is simultaneously the means you acquire the state of, say your fingers, as well as the means by which you update their state by flexing, clenching, pinching, etc. Since all finite beings have finite attention, if you can feel into another's orientation and thereby subtly shift it, you can control what they observe. If you can control what they observe you will shift how they re-orient, decide, and act. But note when feeling into another OODA loop, if that loop can learn and adapt it will naturally develop reciprocal feeling right back into you in order to exert/preserve their own homeostatic control.

Feeling is high signal. It doesn't involve narrative, or extensive data analysis, it is immediate and actionable. Feeling is fundamentally an error signal, not a full description ([Friston's free-energy principle](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2787): a system models its world and learns only from the gap between prediction and outcome). What that implies is to feel is to both predict the future state of the other OODA loop as well as receive feedback when that prediction is violated. The rider expects their horse to travel where pointed. They also expect the horse to eventually get tired. Holding these two expectations together shows how surprise can be either a pleasant or an unpleasant experience, and sometimes both simultaneously.

Normativity is not shared between loops. Fundamentally if one loop maintains decision making capability it controls its own normativity. That is different from controlling its behavior. It is possible to understand another's normativity without sharing it. This enables you to influence that loop with information-optimal modeling properties.

Corollary. Influence can be consentual or covert. These observations do not protect against covert influence, but do allow us to name it more precisely.

Mutual Benefit. For feeling to be mutual, both parties must be invested in maintaining the connection. For two learning OODA loops to feel into each other, they must mutually benefit enough to afford the communication cost. Running a horse too hard for too long destroys trust and makes the relationship adversarial.

Right feel is scale agnostic but not scale invariant. By this I mean its implementation is scale specific, but its principles transcend those scales. Feel negotiates between two loops: self and other, but not between the constituent loops of the other. This is also why sight-feel, sound-feel, touch-feel, etc have such different phenomenology.

Feeling is a specific flavor of affordance available to a learning loop. There are three unique purposes for generalized affordances External-Internal (perception) messaging; Internal-External (intention) messaging; and Internal-Internal (proprioception) messaging. To be a horse or a rider, each can collapse their feeling into either perception or intention, but to be a horseback rider part of what it 'feels' is proprioceptive feedback between the horse and the rider. Part of what it 'intends' is proprioceptive feedback from from the rider's movements into the horse.

Venkatesh Rao and the Protocol Institute introduced the term [New Nature](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/new-nature) to describe "Regimes of reality governed by technologically mediated laws that are nearly as inviolable, immutable, and persistent as those of nature itself." New Nature is here. It has been here for your entire life,
but it is quickly growing in fecundity. Mastery used to resemble that of the swordsman more so than the rider. Our tools didn't think back so much as they do now. So the question of how do you extend your embodiment through felt sense is all the more important.

New nature is 'nature' because we can observe the building blocks of life within it, there are OODA loops being born and evolving. What New Nature doesn't answer is what, why, or how these entities feel. We have embodied experience that lets us infer what, why, and how it is to feel naturally, but this does not extend neo-naturally into our roads, thought-works, and mechanisms. The realization that we live within new nature provides the possibility of embodying new nature as well. If we reject this opportunity we guarantee to live in a world of ghosts and demons, for some metaphenomenon will surely direct and control the orientation of the OODA loops as they evolve and proliferate. By leaning into new nature, and learning to re-embody ourselves within this new substrate we can direct its vitality to be congruent with the messy diverse world of our society, co-adapting with it. Similar to invasive species, an ecosystem can either be supplanted by, or co-adapt the invader. Eradication is inefficient and ineffective.

The protocol institute's study of New Nature is couched in terms of identifying don't-be-evil vs can't-be-evil dynamics. That is interesting but not primary to what I'm pointing at. Instead, observe that we are already located within new nature and original-nature. New nature is inviolable, immutable and persistent in the way hunger and death are, not in the way gravity and electromagnetism are. We are operating within a new substrate and it is becoming part of the ecosystem we're embedded-in and embodied-by. In essence it is our new ecosystem, encapsulating the old landscape and extending beyond it.

Ethan Mollick and colleagues define two effective strategies for AI assisted workflows as becoming either a centaur or a cyborg:

> Centaur work has a clear line between person and machine, like the clear line between the human torso and horse body of the mythical centaur. Centaurs have a strategic division of labor, switching between AI and human tasks, allocating responsibilities based on the strengths and capabilities of each entity. ... Cyborgs blend machine and person, integrating the two deeply. Cyborgs don't just delegate tasks; they intertwine ... so that Cyborgs find themselves working in tandem with the AI.

([Centaurs and Cyborgs on the Jagged Frontier](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/centaurs-and-cyborgs-on-the-jagged)). 

A centaur's notional control is much more situated within the person, whereas the cyborg strategy spreads that out, diluting the person across the breadth of the AI assisted workflow. The centaur integrates, the cyborg assimilates. They both operate from different principles, but principles are not the same as efficacy. These strategies clearly have different properties in terms of their OODA loops and understanding those differences is the key to becoming both principled and effective.

Two cultural exemplars of what it means to be a centaur or cyborg are [Chiron](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Chiron-Greek-mythology) and [The Borg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borg).

Chiron is the half brother of Zeus, born from Chronos and a water nymph Philyra. Chiron was raised by Apollo because his mother rejected him when she saw his monstrous half-human half-beast appearance. Unlike most centaurs, which are described as bestial and savage, Chiron is described as supremely wise and is the teacher of many of the heroes of greek mythology: Heracles, Achilles and his father Peleus, as well as Jason of the Argonauts. Although immortal, he renounces his immortality and grants it to Prometheus. In recognition he was re-immortalized as the constellation Centaurus. 

Key traits emerge from the character of Chiron. He lives in the liminal space between savagery and deliberation. Although birthed from an act of trauma, he doesn't propagate that trauma. He directs his gifts towards the advancement of others. Paradoxically he rejects his immortality and thereby receives it. Chiron is principally an _integrator_, able to keep his parts distinct yet inseparable.

The Borg are much more contemporary, yet just as mythologically important. Where Chiron integrates, The Borg _assimilate_. In the Star Trek universe, these entities do not have a clear home or origin. They travel through the galaxy, overwhelming and absorbing cultures into their collective. They take the bodies and unique skills of their conquests and assimilate them into their body of drones. A borg drone is similar to an ant, it has no identity outside that of the collective. This collective is an egregore. There is no real head. Killing one organ will not incapacitate the body. The drone's cyborg enhancements give them each superhuman abilities that are shared by and indistinguishable from its collective siblings. Its skin is grey, and nanoprobes patrol its bloodstream, rewiring the host organism to the will of the collective at the deepest of levels.

The drone no longer communicates or collaborates with the collective, they are a simple antenna into the whims and vagaries of its ephemeral heart. The grey skin reflects the drone's loss of vitality. It is no longer a being, it is a simple instrument.

Whereas any being can become the Borg, there can only be one Chiron. He is unique in his arc, his prowess, and his relationships. Centaurs preserve their individual identity and propagate heroes and their stories. We can't be Chiron, but we can be like him.

It is this choice between integration and assimilation that characterizes the difference between a centaur and a cyborg. It's not what electromechanical implants a person has, it is their relation to them. A centaur integrates capabilities, accepting their own internal autonomy and creating integrated capacity through mutual modeling and its resulting mutual trust. A cyborg takes capabilities and dissolves their identity into the mix. Both start with the same capabilities; what differs is how control and communication are distributed.

New nature is burgeoning. We are embarking on a new Cambrian era defined by the new life forms that are emerging. New nature is equivalent to the introduction of multicellular life on earth. And just as multicellular life existed before the Cambrian era, the forms of New nature existed before this moment but it was slow and sluggish. We rode horses, we assembled ourselves into nations and corporations. But now it's becoming undeniable that my identity is not just my body, just as the identity of my eyeball is not just that of an eyeball. Nervous systems are growing. They are connecting different parts, forming bodies. The nature of the nervous system is a protocol for feeling. Out of feeling comes thought. The types of thought formed by that protocol can favor Chiron-like or Borg-like thoughts. Does _our_ new nature have a spine or an exoskeleton or give rise to a sponge-like amorphous body?

# Part II: Sketching a Protocol For Proprioception in New Nature

## Purpose

Part I made a claim about feeling. This part asks what it would take to build the substrate that makes feeling possible---not feeling itself, which develops in the practitioner, through training, over years, but the minimum protocol such that an organ can be felt rather than numb.

A note on words before the protocol begins. Throughout Part II, "feeling" names an affordance an interface provides to a practitioner who feels---never a claim that the substrate feels. When I write that an organ can be "felt rather than numb," the felt sense lives in the practitioner; "numb" names an interface that gives that sense nothing to grip. The protocol engineers the affordance; the phenomenology stays where Part I left it, in the wielder, not the blade.

A protocol is neither a product nor an architecture. It dictates neither the shape of an organ nor its function. It specifies only what must pass between organs, in which directions, and what must remain traversable so that feeling can form. The wager is Rao's: a protocol that makes feeling *structural*---can't-be-evil---will out-navigate any product feature that merely asks its participants to feel responsibly---don't-be-evil. Not because it is more virtuous, but because a body that preserves communication between nested control-loci is more information and adaptation efficient than one that cannot.

Part I's inner and outer loops, referenced in observation three, are the spine. Three axes follow:

1. **Identifying and growing organs** --- the substrate: what an organ is, what it must declare, and the wiring that lets feeling travel.
2. **Protocols for pragmatic feeling** --- the inner loop: operating through a held model of the other and surfacing surprise. Feeling as it *operates*.
3. **Protocols for epistemic feeling** --- the outer loop: revising the model and the control space itself, and the collective machinery by which a body learns and speciates. Feeling as it *learns*.

The pragmatic/epistemic pairing is deliberate: it is the same exploit/explore split that [active inference](https://doi.org/10.1080/17588928.2015.1020053) names *pragmatic* and *epistemic* value---the inner loop spends a held model to get somewhere, the outer loop spends action to improve the model itself.


## Axis 1: Identifying and Growing Organs --- The Substrate

An organ is an OODA loop you have chosen to embody: separable from you, possessed of its own decision-making capacity, yet imbued with feeling in both directions. The design question is not "what can this capability do?" but "where is its locus of control, and can I feel across the boundary?" A capability whose motive force sits entirely inside your own loop is not an organ but a tool---the sword, the motorboat. An organ is the horse: it acts on its own, and embodiment is the ongoing negotiation Part I described.

### What an organ declares: the normativity model

To feel into an organ you need a reliable model of its normativity---not a mechanistic account of how it works, but a model of how it *decides*. ([Conant and Ashby](https://doi.org/10.1080/00207727008920220) proved the general form: every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system. The refinement here is that the model can be of the system's *normativity*---its policy---rather than its full dynamics.) Part I located normativity in the OODA "decide" step: an ordering over possible actions. An organ makes itself felt by declaring that ordering as an interface with four surfaces:

- **Observes** --- what it can perceive (its external-internal surface).
- **Orients** --- what it attends to, the frame it holds. This is where you inject; the tack point.
- **Decides** --- its action space and the ordering over it: its values, made explicit.
- **Acts** --- what it can effect (its internal-external surface).

The normativity lives in the *decide* surface, and it is not a thing but a decision: the policy by which the organ, having read the wind, sets its observation, orientation, and action into one configuration rather than another. No single part carries it---it is the captain's choice, not the keel or the rudder. An organ with no such policy is a leaf in the wind; an organ whose policy is opaque is numb, and you cannot feel where to tack.

### Procedures: the declaration made testable

A normativity model is inert until it commits to decisions. A **procedure** is a testable decision rule: *in context Y, take action X, expecting result Z*. The commitment to X in context Y exposes the organ's decision criteria---its values in motion---and the predicted Z makes that commitment falsifiable, surfacing a redline when reality diverges. Not only the practitioner's criteria: the organization's, the technology's.

A procedure exposes the *decide* layer, and only that layer. How the organ detects context Y (observe), what frame makes X salient (orient), and how X is carried out (act) remain background the implementor supplies and the user infers. This is the right transparency between the inner and outer loops: the inner loop runs the procedure on its held criteria, the outer loop revises those criteria when the predictions fail, and neither side needs a mechanistic account of how the organ observes or acts. Procedures make the captain's policy legible without exposing the rigging that carries it out---which is exactly Part I's claim that you steer by a model of the other's normativity, not by a map of its mechanism.

### Organs as living patterns

An organ's pattern is forkable, not imposed---an Alexandrian pattern, not a specification. The pattern is tended by an epistemic collective, a guild, whose role is to explore and maintain its normative potential, not collectively capture its instrumental use. Each instantiation is unique to its host. The host owns the pragmatic instance and captures value through it, while owing the guild epistemic feedback: redlines, adaptations, blazed trails. Ownership is federated---pull-based adoption, no central instance through which all value must pass.

### Bi-directional traceability: the nervous tissue

A connection between organs must be traversable from both ends. An organ must feel what depends on it (*used-by*) and what it depends on (*uses*). One reference, written once, but readable from both sides---the work of back-links, join tables, linkers, compilers, and DNS registries. If only one side can traverse the connection, then only a central authority can see the whole, and that authority *is* the capture surface.

Bi-directional traceability is the can't-be-evil mechanism, and it runs on two axes:

- **Across space** --- whom I depend on, who depends on me. This is what makes opt-out and forking *safe*: you can exit without invisible breakage only if you can trace what your contribution holds up and what holds it up.
- **Across time** --- who I was. An organ's lineage of adaptations stays traversable, so its trajectory can be inspected---smooth deformation told apart from a jump. This is how continuity, and thus (as Axis 3 shows) the line between integration and assimilation, becomes auditable.

An organ that only receives commands from above, with no traceable feeling flowing back, is a drone. An organ wired both ways is a participant. Bi-directionality is the precondition for everything that follows.

### Where to look

The organs most worth growing are the liminal ones, where the boundary is actively negotiated rather than settled: your role inside an organization, an open-source project you contribute to, a shared car that cannot feel whose principal to respect, your children. These are the sites where proprioception is hardest and most consequential, because self and other have not yet been decided.

## Axis 2: Protocols for Pragmatic Feeling --- The Inner Loop

The inner loop holds the model of the other fixed and acts through it. You assume the wind is constant for the length of the tack. This is the exploit regime: given what I believe about this organ's normativity, steer. Pragmatic feeling is what lets that steering happen without deliberation---the felt sense of the driver who knows how hard to brake without thinking about it.

### Redlines: the error signal

Part I established that feeling is an error signal, not a full description: you predict the other's next state and receive feedback only on violation. The protocol primitive for that violation is the **redline**---a surfaced surprise. Its envelope:

- **source** --- which loop emitted it.
- **referent** --- the procedure whose prediction failed, cited by reference. Both parties already hold the procedure, so the redline compresses against shared context.
- **divergence** --- predicted versus observed: the surprise itself.

Three fields. Direction is implicit: the (source, referent) tuple tells you which loop is surprised and which procedure is in question. A soldier's redline on a battlefield procedure surfaces surprise upward to the commander; the commander sharing a redline against the same procedure orients the soldier downward. Same envelope, same channel, direction resolved by who sent it.

A redline is routable only because connections are bi-directionally traceable (Axis 1); without back-links, surprise has nowhere to flow but up to a center. The protocol carries divergence, never normativity. The divergence is a coordinate-free vector: each receiver resolves it into its own coordinate system---projecting it onto its own values---to read what the surprise means for it. There is no normativity to ship, only implicit in the shared procedure that the redline references. A redline is not failure, it is evidence. Acute redlines resolve through adaptation. A chronic redline---the same divergence surfacing again and again---signals that the model itself is wrong, and hands control up to the outer loop.

### The constraints that let feeling form

Three constraints on the interface, or the nerve stays dead:

- **Learnable coupling.** The same action must yield feedback you can *learn from*---which is not the same as feedback that never changes, nor even feedback that is merely stable. A stable coupling is the sword's degenerate case: learnable once, then fixed, with nothing left to track. An organ that models you back asks more, and this is the subtle part---*adaptation* is fine, even necessary: it answers the same action differently as it learns you, so the surface link from input to response is non-stationary by design. Noise is fine too; reality is noisy. What must stay learnable is one level up---the *normativity* generating the response, the policy you are actually modeling (Observation Three). The horse that spooks today and settles tomorrow runs the same horse-nature throughout; you can track the change because the thing beneath it holds still, or deforms smoothly. The fatal case is the coupling that *confounds by design*: a slot machine is perfectly consistent---a fixed distribution---yet answers your pulls with just enough apparent pattern to keep you modeling and never enough to let the model pay. The felt sense you form is a phantom it farms. So the diagnostic is not stability but whether modeling *pays*: does your surprise actually fall as you model? Honest noise settles to a floor you can feel against; a rigged coupling holds your prediction error high while dressing it as near-mastery. A redline must mean the same thing each time it fires---it must point at a normativity you can hold, not at a pattern designed to dissolve the moment you reach for it.
- **Invisibility.** Normal operation produces no signal (Whitehead: a tool advances by becoming unnoticed). You do not feel digestion; you feel hunger. Proprioception is felt sense *into the invisible*---surfacing the organ only on surprise, then receding. An organ that demands constant attention is not yet embodied.
- **Three orientations.** The interface must carry all three of Part I's directions: external-internal (perception), internal-external (intention), and internal-internal (proprioception). Most interfaces collapse to the first two---dashboards and command lines. Proprioception, the felt coordination *between* organs, is the one usually missing, and it is the one that makes a body out of a pile of parts.

### Knowing the inner loop works

Two questions diagnose pragmatic feeling. **Coherence**: faced with the same circumstance, will two practitioners reach for the same action? Divergence here measures whether causality is consistent across the body. **Fidelity**: how finely does the organ respond to small changes in what it perceives? Too coarse and the practitioner cannot feel through it; too twitchy and every gust reads as a redline.

## Axis 3: Protocols for Epistemic Feeling --- The Outer Loop

Winds change and goals change. The outer loop is where the held model, and the control space itself, get revised---the explore regime. It is also where the choice between Chiron and the Borg is actually decided, because both update their goals in the wind. The difference is not whether you change; it is whether the change preserves a self.

### Control-space continuity: the test that separates integration from assimilation

Identity is not the keel, nor the heading, nor any single decision---it is the *control space* itself: the coordinate system in which an organ's observations, decisions, and actions are expressed and made to mean something. Decisions are vectors within that space; values are an ordering over it; the *you* is the space, the basis you resolve the world into. A centaur re-trims its keel---new heading, new configuration, new decisions---while its control space deforms smoothly, each adaptation a small self-directed move that leaves it reachable from who it was. A Borg has its control space *overwritten* by the collective's: not a deformation but a jump, a teleport into a foreign coordinate system. The discriminator is *continuity* in the strict sense: are the transitions smooth? Integration is a connected trajectory through the space of control spaces; assimilation is a loss of ownership of this control space. This is why the overwrite is such a violation---not because the change is large, but because present-you cannot smoothly associate with past-you normatively. Will is preserved by continuity, not stasis: you can replace every plank, grow new dimensions, rotate the whole basis, and keep acting as one agent, so long as the path stays connected. A protocol that keeps the control space evolving smoothly---no cliffs, no overwrites---preserves its will. That is the can't-be-evil form of Chiron's Law.

Preserving will is not the same as preserving identity, and this essay does not pretend to settle the harder question. The primitives are enough to *pose* it: continuity guards the will, but whether it also guards the self---whether a slow walk that keeps your will intact while bending your values until you hold the opposite tack still leaves *you* standing---is a question we open rather than close. Continuity is necessary for identity; it is plainly not sufficient. And the gap is not merely philosophical: it is an attack surface. A value-inverting walk can be driven not by your own decide-surface but from outside, through the orient surface Axis 1 called the tack point---the cult, the radicalization pipeline, the recommender that authors your preferences one self-consistent step at a time. Every step is small and locally reachable, the lineage trace shows no teleport, and so the across-time audit, which only tells smooth from jagged, is blind to it. The near-term claim is therefore the modest one: the protocol forecloses assimilation by *overwrite*, the sudden jump into a foreign control space; the slow walk it can name but not yet prevent.

### What lets revision happen: grace and progressive engagement

The outer loop runs only if the practitioner can own a failed model instead of disowning it. **Grace before feedback**: shame says "that wasn't really me" and dissociates the practitioner from the organ---the redline is rejected, the model never updates, inflammation turns chronic. Grace says "that was me, running a model that failed," which keeps the signal owned and the loop alive. Grace is the anti-inflammatory.

And capacity has to be reachable. **Progressive engagement**: the interface must be enterable at the shallow end and deepen without a cliff---sit the horse, then walk, then trot, then charge. If there is a wall between assisted use and taking control, proprioception never crosses it and the user stays a passenger. This is Chiron's Law as an interface constraint: build the rider's capacity, do not fight the rider's fights. Its measure is **comprehensibility**---the distance, in effort, from passenger to rider.

### Capture resistance by construction

Rao's Central Dogma: there is no mechanism for wise human regulation that does not also open an attack surface for capture. Every point of discretion is a place where power can preferentially attach. The protocol's defense is to replace discretion with structure wherever it can---and the structure is, again, bi-directional traceability.

This reframes the familiar exit rights. **Opt-out** (leave without losing your belief network or trail) and **forking** (take the pattern and instantiate a community with a different orientation) are not themselves the capture resistance; alone, they are promises. They become structural only because traceability guarantees you can leave without invisible breakage, and the guild cannot quietly make your contributions load-bearing in ways you cannot see. The guild's standing temptations---redefining the shared aspiration by fiat, taxing perception, retaining members by holding their credentials hostage, rent-seeking between communities---are all attempts to manufacture a capture surface. A numb connection is exactly where capture hides, and the protocol forbids numb connections.

The propagation argument is the one that carried public-key cryptography: capture resistance spreads not because it is principled but because it works. I hypothesize that a body that feels its organs adapts faster than a numb one. When conditions change, feeling provides the necessary evolutionary adaptation signal; the principles come along for the ride.

### Weirdness as signal

Tools that sharpen *perception* increase weirdness; tools that sharpen *control* decrease it. The outer loop runs on the former. Your deviation from the collective model is not error to be corrected toward the mean---it is the raw material of speciation. The collective's externalized model must therefore show *variance*, not just an average: when a large fraction of practitioners redline at the same step, the organization's model is wrong, not the practitioners. Blazing a trail no one has walked is the contribution; the collective folds your path into the space of paths others can take. This is how a skill tree stays generative rather than predefined.

### Scale-free, with a boundary

The same protocol serves the cell, the organ, the organism, and the organization; inhabit a role and you become a constituent loop in a larger body, same interface at a different scale. But Part I drew a hard boundary: feeling is dyadic. It negotiates between self and other; it does not reach through the other into the other's constituents. Nesting is therefore recursive, not transparent---you feel your organ, you do not feel your organ's organs. This boundary is not a limitation to engineer away. It is what keeps a body from becoming an egregore. A head exists only where feeling is asymmetric and stops at a boundary; a body whose feeling penetrated uniformly through every scale would have no locus of responsibility at all. The protocol keeps the head precisely by refusing to dissolve the boundaries between scales.

### Closing

A nervous system is a protocol for feeling, and the protocol you build decides the kind of body that can grow on it. Wire feeling to flow both ways, across space and across time; make surprise cheap to surface and models cheap to revise; refuse the numb connection wherever it would form. Do that, and the body can stay a centaur---its parts distinct yet inseparable, its control space its own---as it sails into winds that did not exist a moment ago. Fail, and the winds will choose the harbor.

# Appendix A: What Counts as an Organ?

Formally, an organ is an epistemic community --- a network of individuals contributing to a body of knowledge related to a shared aspiration. The aspiration defines the community, and participation implies commitment to the common goal.

This is the same object Axis 1 called an OODA loop you embody, seen from the collective side. An organ is *epistemic* because it cares about a particular facet of reality---its normativity is a claim about which slice of the world matters, and how. That caring is its control space: the coordinate system in which a few distinctions are salient and most are not. A community forms around a facet because exploring it is more than any one member can do alone.

The caring is what gives an organ its two flavors, and they map to the two loops. To **embody** an organ is to capture its *pragmatic* value: instantiate the pattern in your own context and steer by it---the inner loop, exploiting the facet the organ already understands. To **grow** an organ is *epistemic*: to explore the state space its normativity opens, striking into regions of the facet no one has mapped---the outer loop, feeding what you find back to the community. A person embodies; a guild grows; and because both relate to the same control space, the pragmatic instance and the epistemic exploration stay coupled. The catalogue below sorts organs by whose felt-body they extend, but every one is both: a facet some community cares about, and a capability some practitioner embodies.

**Personal organs** (extensions of individual felt-body):
- **Phones** --- observes your location, activity, and attention; orients to engagement models; decides what to surface and when. You feel its acts (notifications) but not its orient (what it suppresses and why). Partially numb: the inner loop runs on your side, but the phone's normativity is opaque.
- Social media avatars
- Operating systems
- Email/calendar
- **Financial accounts** --- observe transactions, orient to risk/return models, decide trades and recurring payments autonomously. You feel the acts (balance changes) but rarely the orient (credit scoring model, fraud heuristics). The redline arrives as a declined transaction—too late to revise the model that produced it.
- Health/fitness wearables
- Skill sets and craft knowledge (accumulated capacity you can't fully articulate—the normativity is embodied, never declared)

**Collective organs** (extensions of organizational felt-body):
- Corporate reporting trees
- Degrees/credentials
- Internal networks
- **Codebases** --- observe commits, orient to architectural conventions, resist changes that violate them, act through compiler errors and test failures. Bi-directional traceability exists (git blame, dependency graphs) but the orient surface is rarely declared explicitly. The normativity is implicit in code style and convention, not stated—which is where technical debt hides.
- **Legal/regulatory frameworks** --- observe behavior, orient to precedent, decide enforcement. The decide surface is declared (statutes) but the orient surface (prosecutorial discretion, regulatory interpretation) is opaque. Capture hides in the gap between declared decides and undeclared orients.
- Supply chains
- Shared knowledge bases / wikis
- CI/CD pipelines and monitoring

**Liminal organs** (boundary actively negotiated --- most ripe for disruption):
- **Smart homes** --- multiple principals. The thermostat observes aggregate behavior but cannot orient to which occupant's normativity to respect. Learnable coupling fails across users: the same action (adjusting temperature) produces different feedback depending on invisible state (whose model the system learned from). The inner loop cannot form because the organ is numb to whose body it belongs to.
- **Shared cars** --- same multi-principal problem. Seat position, mirrors, driving style all orient to learned preferences, but whose? Each driver's inner loop is disrupted by the residue of another's.
- **Your role within an organization** --- you are simultaneously your own control space AND a constituent loop in the org's. The boundary between inner loop (your craft) and outer loop (org's strategy) is where feeling is most contested. This is where the centaur/cyborg choice is made daily.
- **Your children** --- autonomous OODA loops you helped create. Deeply felt, not fully within your control. The outer loop is lifelong: your model of their normativity must continuously revise as they develop their own control space. The feeling is bi-directional but asymmetric—and the asymmetry shifts over time.
- Collaborative relationships (partnerships, marriages)
- Professional reputation
