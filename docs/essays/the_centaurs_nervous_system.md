Imagine learning how to wield a sword. When you start, there is a sword, and there is you. They are separate and you feel them as separate. At this stage you are nearly as likely to injure yourself as you are to strike your target. Now imagine training with this instrument for years. You start to know the position of the sword without having to see it. You can feel through the sword and intimately know the force a particular strike will impart. Are you and the sword separate? Where the beginner acts with the sword, the master acts through the sword---they relate to the sword as an extension of their will instad of engaging with the sword consiously. Both your internal world as well as your physical musculature and nervous system have adapted and adopted this extension. You are materially different compared to who you were before you started training.

One step further. Imagine you are a knight in training. A knight is dangerous not so much
because of their sword, but because they are a mounted warrior. The horse and the man unite to
become a much more dangerous entity than either on their own. But once again we are faced
with the same progression. When you begin, your steed is separate from yourself. You are
just as likely to injure yourself at this stage as to move and operate according to your intentions.
You must train a long time before horse and identity are united.

The master horseback rider and the master swordsman both have changed their physical and mental identities. But the means and matter of their respective transformations are very different. For a horseback rider to be effective, both the horse and the rider must develop mutual knowledge and trust. For the swordsman to be effective they must know the nature of their blade. Whatever possible awareness within the blade is moot. The action of the wielder solely determines the blade's position. Whereas the horse and rider have an ongoing negotiation on who is in charge, whose will leads and whose will follows.

What distinguishes both these masters from beginners is how their felt sense extends through and beyond their body into their instrument. They have subconscious and pre-analytical feedback from the sword, from the horse. Relative to their motives, they can feel what movement is "right" or "wrong".

The OODA loop, coined by Colonel John Boyd points out how some entities, like humans and horses, observe, orient, decide, and act (OODA) in an ongoing dynamic loop. Some entities, like swords and hammers, do not. But critically, living beings are composed of countless numbers of such loops. Each cell has numerous OODA loops, each organ, perhaps each brain hemisphere its own. Yet within that cacophony is you. Multiple OODA loops do not define what is or isn't you, instead how they relate---who is "inside" the other's loop determines where the locus of action lives.

But inside doesn't mean what you think it means. A cell is physically inside me, but my cellular OODA loops are not 'inside' my phenomenology. What determines who is inside versus who is outside is not a question of frequency or material size. What is inside vs outside is a question of whose principles are most respected. Boyd didn't realize it to my knowledge but what it means to be inside means to be _normatively inside_ another loop. Let me explain.

Each loop is inherently faced with many possible decisions. The 'decide' component tells us that within the loop there's the possibility space of more than one possible action, and an ordering of those possibilities that culminates into action. How they decide is an internal preference that constitutes a big piece of their identity. Yet _how_ they decide is different than _what_ they decide. What's decided is a ranking of possible next states based on what's immediately available. This is how one loop can be 'inside' the other; By exerting influence over what another 'sees' as available.

Influencing what another loop decides is not the same as deciding _for_ the loop, because inherent in the concept of observing another loop is it is other -- its processes are external to yours. So being normatively inside another is to influence it by changing what it pays attention to, not to consume it. By changing what it pays attention to, the 'normatively inside' loop recursively influences what the other loop observes, where it orients, and therefore what it decides and enacts. It is entirely feasible for a smaller, slower entity to be inside the other's OODA loop if they can change what another loop pays attention to. When one entity can feel into the other's loop, correctly anticipating when why and how their OODA will function, they can extend Tai Chi's effortless effort---the directing of external force according to their principles.

Note that we have a built in understanding that feeling is normative. We all have first hand experience of how much of our inner lives are value laden: this tastes good/bad; that looks beautiful or abhorrent, etc. But we can start to answer why. Feeling is the negotiation between how we think something we're connected to _should_ act according to our principles versus how we observe it acting.

This is what determines the difference between a beginning rider and experienced cavalry. Going into battle is fearful. A skillful rider must accept that fear and acknowledge it. They must predict its limits and console the fear of two warm-blooded animals face-to-face with an existential threat. Whether you charge or balk is a question of who you are. Are you the horse? Yes. Are you the rider? Yes. When you accept you are both and neither then you can orient both towards what you value instead of what scares you.

The rider points to another important fact related to multi OODA loop mastery. Control can have loci, but where such loci are situated is scale dependent in a way that optimizes local processing speed. This is what separates effective organizations (and organisms) from ineffective ones. The rider is "within" the horse's OODA loop in a manner that is effectively different than how the horse internalizes the rider. In a multi OODA loop body, each OODA loop must develop "right feel" for the others. The rider feels the horse, the horse feels the rider. For them to be martially effective, each must develop right feel and _right_ depends on subjective perspective.

What does the master drone operator embody? Do the autonomous swarms form an extension of her will, or is her agency diluted and buffeted without firm control of whose OODA loop is inside whose. Is it the company that made the AI targeting software inside her loop or is she inside theirs? What role do the chips and hardware which constitute each drone play? Is it the general whose command told the operator to attack? Perhaps the drones themselves are in control? The answer to this question is an OODA loop question. Who is most inside whom? And the answer to that question is best couched as one of proprioception. Whoever has a firmer 'felt sense' of the other can use that sense to effectively extend effortless effort to direct their energy. When there is no such felt sense, or the sense of feeling is spread uniformly through the OODA actors, an egregore appears. Such spirits show themselves when control is so diluted that the body has no functioning head, no entity to claim responsibility for the principles enacted.

War usefully describes these phenomena because it paint actions and intention in such stark outlines. In essence, war is a cartoon of reality. There is us, there is them, there is our power, and theirs. But please note how this story transcends warfare and extends throughout our ancient and modern lives. The sword can just as easily be a pen; The horse can just as easily be a pet; the drone an autonomous car. What's important is the meditation on what it means to be me, given that I can change who I am purely based on what I can feel.

But what does right feel consist of? I cannot pretend to offer a complete answer this question, but we can observe some properties by breaking down 'right' and 'feel'.

Feeling is multi-directional. Unlike mechanistic descriptions of cause and effect, feeling describes both the sense of intentional movement as well as sensation of external stimuli. Feeling is simultaneously the means you acquire the state of, say your fingers, as well as the means by which you update their state by flexing, clenching, pinching, etc. 

Since all finite beings have finite attention, if you can feel into another's orientation you gain insight into how and why it might shift. If you can subtly shift it, you can control what they observe. If you can control what they observe, you will shift how they re-orient, decide, and act. But look, feeling into an entity that can can learn and adapt it will naturally develop reciprocal feeling right back into you in order to exert/preserve their own homeostatic control.

Feeling is high signal. It doesn't involve narrative, or extensive data analysis, it is immediate and actionable. Feeling is fundamentally an error signal, not a full description ([Friston's free-energy principle](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2787): a system models its world and learns only from the gap between prediction and outcome). What that implies is to feel is to both predict the future state of the other OODA loop as well as receive feedback when that prediction is violated. The rider expects their horse to travel where pointed. They also expect the horse to eventually get tired. Holding these two expectations together shows how surprise can be either a pleasant or an unpleasant experience, and sometimes both simultaneously.

Normativity is not shared between loops. Fundamentally if one loop keeps its own will it controls its own normativity. That is different from controlling its behavior. It is possible to understand another's normativity without sharing it. This enables you to influence that loop with information-optimal modeling properties.

Corollary. Influence can be consentual or covert. These observations do not protect against covert influence, but do allow us to name it more precisely.

Mutual Benefit. For feeling to be mutual, both parties must be invested in maintaining the connection. For two learning OODA loops to feel into each other, they must mutually benefit enough to afford the communication cost. Running a horse too hard for too long destroys trust and makes the relationship adversarial.

Right feel is scale agnostic but not scale invariant. By this I mean its implementation is scale specific, but its principles transcend those scales. Feel negotiates between two loops: self and other, but not between the constituent loops of the other. This is also why sight-feel, sound-feel, touch-feel, etc have such different phenomenology.

Feeling is a specific flavor of affordance available to a learning loop. There are three unique purposes for generalized affordances External-Internal (perception) messaging; Internal-External (intention) messaging; and Internal-Internal (proprioception) messaging. To be a horse or a rider, each can collapse their feeling into either perception or intention, but to be a horseback rider part of what it 'feels' is proprioceptive feedback between the horse and the rider. Part of what the rider intends is channeled as proprioceptive feedback from from the rider's movements into and through the horse.

Venkatesh Rao and the Protocol Institute introduced the term [New Nature](https://contraptions.venkateshrao.com/p/new-nature) to describe "Regimes of reality governed by technologically mediated laws that are nearly as inviolable, immutable, and persistent as those of nature itself." New Nature is here. It has been here for your entire life,
but it is quickly growing in fecundity. Mastery used to resemble that of the swordsman more so than the rider. Our tools didn't used to think back. So the question of how do you extend your embodiment through feeling into relationships that have their own OODA loops is all the more important.

New nature is 'nature' because we can observe the building blocks of life within it, there are OODA loops being born and evolving. What New Nature doesn't answer is what, why, or how these entities feel. We have embodied experience that lets us infer what, why, and how it is to feel naturally, but this does not extend neo-naturally into our roads, thought-works, and automatations. The realization that we live within new nature provides the possibility of embodying new nature as well. If we reject the possibility of feeling into these structures we guarantee to live in a world of ghosts and demons, for some metaphenomenon will surely direct the orientation of new nature's OODA loops as they evolve and proliferate. By leaning into new nature, and learning to re-embody ourselves within this new substrate we can direct its vitality to be congruent with the messy diverse world of our society, co-adapting with it. Similar to invasive species, an ecosystem can either be supplanted by, or co-adapt the invader. Eradication is never the low energy option.

The protocol institute's study of New Nature is couched in terms of identifying don't-be-evil vs can't-be-evil dynamics. That is interesting but not primary to what I'm pointing at. Instead, observe that we are already located within new nature as well as original-nature. New nature is inviolable, immutable and persistent in the way hunger and death are, not in the way gravity and electromagnetism are. We can ignore them for a while, but we pay a high interest to for ignoring their force. We are operating within a new substrate and it is becoming part of the ecosystem we're embedded-in and embodied-by. In essence it is our new ecosystem, encapsulating the old landscape and extending beyond it.

Ethan Mollick and colleagues define two effective strategies for AI assisted workflows as becoming either a centaur or a cyborg:

> Centaur work has a clear line between person and machine, like the clear line between the human torso and horse body of the mythical centaur. Centaurs have a strategic division of labor, switching between AI and human tasks, allocating responsibilities based on the strengths and capabilities of each entity. ... Cyborgs blend machine and person, integrating the two deeply. Cyborgs don't just delegate tasks; they intertwine ... so that Cyborgs find themselves working in tandem with the AI.

([Centaurs and Cyborgs on the Jagged Frontier](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/centaurs-and-cyborgs-on-the-jagged)). 

A centaur's notional control is much more situated within the person, whereas the cyborg strategy spreads that out, diluting the person across the breadth of the AI assisted workflow. The centaur integrates, the cyborg assimilates. They both operate from different principles, but principles are not the same as efficacy. These strategies clearly have different properties in terms of their OODA loops and understanding those differences is the key to becoming both principled and effective.

Two cultural exemplars of what it means to be a centaur or cyborg are [Chiron](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Chiron-Greek-mythology) and [The Borg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borg).

Chiron is the half-brother of Zeus, born of Chronos and the water nymph Philyra, and raised by Apollo after his mother — repulsed by his half-human, half-beast form — abandoned him. Unlike his savage, bestial kin, Chiron is supremely wise: physician and tutor to Heracles, to Achilles and his father Peleus, to Jason of the Argonauts. And here is the knot at his center. This master healer carries a wound that will not heal. Struck by accident with one of Heracles' arrows, its tip steeped in the Hydra's venom, the immortal Chiron is left in ceaseless agony — pain he can soothe in others but never in himself. His only release is to *give his deathlessness away*: he trades his immortality to free Prometheus, and is at last permitted to die. In recognition he is hung in the sky as the constellation Centaurus.

Key traits emerge. He lives in the liminal space between savagery and deliberation; born of trauma, he does not pass it on, but turns his gifts toward others. But the trait the myth places at his core is the one that matters most here: Chiron is the *wounded healer*. His wisdom is not in spite of his wound but bound up with it — he can hold another's pain precisely because he is, incurably, in pain himself.

The Borg are much more contemporary, yet just as mythologically important. Where Chiron integrates, The Borg _assimilate_. In the Star Trek universe, these entities do not have a clear home or origin. They travel through the galaxy, overwhelming and absorbing cultures into their collective. They take the bodies and unique skills of their conquests and assimilate them into their body of drones. A borg drone is similar to an ant, it has no identity outside that of the collective. This collective is an egregore. There is no real head. Killing one organ will not incapacitate the body. The drone's cyborg enhancements give them each superhuman abilities that are shared by and indistinguishable from its collective siblings. Its skin is grey, and nanoprobes patrol its bloodstream, rewiring the host organism to the will of the collective at the deepest of levels.

The drone no longer communicates or collaborates with the collective, they are a simple antenna into the whims and vagaries of its ephemeral heart. The grey skin reflects the drone's loss of vitality. It is no longer a being, it is an undead instrument.

Chiron's defining act is to *choose mortality*: to consent to be ended. That is what makes him the exact inverse of the Borg's grey undeath. The Borg buy coherence by assimilation and refuse to die; Chiron's coherence costs him everything and accepts death as its price. He is an _integrator_ but he integrates as one who can be cut, and who can let go. That capacity, to be wounded and to choose an ending, is inseparable from feeling.

Whereas any being can become assimilated into the Borg, there can only be one Chiron. He is one creature of two natures, man and horse, held distinct yet inseparable. His myth is not so much related to his accomplishments but of who he relates to. His body-form symbolizes his principles; strength, integration, relation, health and mortality. Chiron perceives relations according to their current capacity and potential. He knows how to grow capacity into right relation. He brings  figure who has mastered, in his own person, the art it runs on: keeping parts sovereign while moving as one. We cannot be Chiron, but we can practice what he practiced.

It is this choice between integration and assimilation that differentiates a centaur from a cyborg. It's not what electromechanical implants they have, it is their means of relation. A centaur respects external autonomy and creates mutual capacity through modeling the other. A cyborg absorbs capabilities and dissolves them into their identity. Both start with the same relationship; what differs is how they grow that relationship.

New nature is burgeoning. Human civilation is already analogous to multicellular life -- individual persons are to society as mitochondria is to a cell. We are the energy, but what we observe and experiences is constrained by the substrate that civilization proffers to us. New nature whispers that a Cambrian explision is possible in the ways we relate. Do we diversify into new forms of multi-cellularity, growing heterogeneus forms and organs? Or do we become amoeboid -- formless sludge.

Which road our new nature takes is an open question. We rode horses; we assembled ourselves into nations and corporations; but it is now undeniable that my identity is not just my body, just as my eyeball's identity is not just that of an eyeball. Nervous systems are growing — connecting parts into bodies — and a nervous system is a protocol for feeling. Out of feeling comes thought, and the protocol decides whether that thought runs Chiron-like or Borg-like. Does _our_ new nature grow a spine, an exoskeleton, or a sponge-like amorphous body?
