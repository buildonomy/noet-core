title: Feeling Into Right Relation
subtitle: Part II of Cybernetic Embodiment
---

Part I introduced our intuition pump: the sword that becomes an
extension of the hand, the horse that becomes a partner in a shared will, the
drone-swarm whose locus of control no one can quite locate. These examples help evoke what it is to feel, couched in the language of pragmatic and epistemic value. We can start to separate *feeling through* from *feeling into* — the felt sense that reaches across a
boundary into something with its own life on the far side. New Nature has filled the
world with far sides. Models answer back; markets digest their makers; institutions
decide while we sleep. Each is a loop with its own agency, and none of them is alive
in the way you are.

The question Part II asks is not *what body should we build out of these loops.* It
is older and more personal: **how do you hold each new thing in the relation that
fits it?** A sword wants to be a sword. A river is read, not befriended. A child
cannot be commanded and must not be coerced. The faculty that tells these apart —
that lets you meet a tool as a tool and a someone as a someone — is **feeling**. This
part makes three words precise: **right** (the relation a thing's nature affords),
**relation** (the structured space of ways to hold another), and, above all,
**feeling** (the organ that discerns and sustains the fit).

One consequence runs ahead of the argument, and it is worth naming once so it stops
crowding the center: when many loops hold one another in the *partner* relation —
each sovereign, each felt — a body falls out that moves as one without anyone owning
the others. Call it a **felt federation**. It is a real body-form, specified in *The
Policy Surface*. But it is one answer the central question happens to admit, not the
question itself. The question is right relation, and the organ is feeling. Begin
there.

## Feeling through, feeling into

Feeling, in this essay, begins from the **proprioceptive** — the sense by which a
settled body knows its own configuration and acts into it. But proprioception
*proper* is the within-body limit: one blanket, one will, sensing itself. What this
essay is really about lives at the body's *growing edge*, where the boundary is still
in question — where a sword is becoming-extension or a horse becoming-partner — and
there the proprioceptive reaches *past* its single-self home toward something closer
to empathy. Hold proprioception, then, as the limit case the rest is measured
against, not the thing itself. Part I noted feeling runs three directions: *in*
(perception), *out* (intention), and folded *back on itself* (proprioception). What
matters here is that feeling comes in two *depths*, and almost
everything turns on telling them apart.

The shallow depth is feeling **through**. You feel through the sword; tiny signals at
the grip let you model the blade's weight, reach, and the force a strike will land.
This is a *dynamics* model — a good-regulator model of a physical thing's behavior,
a model of how a graspable thing moves and responds. Its surprise falls toward **zero**
as the instrument is mastered. A perfectly learned sword is perfectly transparent: it has become
part of you, and you no longer feel it at all. The master swordsman, Part I's first
figure, lives here.

But going epistemically silent is not the same as being inert. Your relation *to the sword* is purely a matter of knowing: you
stake nothing of the sword's own values (it has nothing to stake), and its model is costless to
revise. Yet in mastering it you write it into your own agency — your reach now routes
through it, and comes to depend on it. That dependence is a stake on *your* side of the
boundary, and it is why losing a mastered extension *cuts*: a piece of your own capacity
left suddenly unreachable (the phantom, below). So the cut has two homes. Feeling-*through*
binds the other into *your* identity — what you can do, what you count on; feeling-*into*, as
the cost section will show, can fold *the other's* principles into your tally of what counts as a win or a loss. The bet tracks only the knowing — where surprise settles — not
this second, harder ledger of stakes.

The deep depth is feeling **into**. You cannot build a dynamics model of a horse's
*will*; its interior is opaque, and no physics predicts a decision. What you build
instead is closer to a mirror-neuron model than to proprioception proper: an
*outcomes* model of the other, grounded not in its mechanics but in its
**normativity** — what it cares about and how it reads the world. You still feel
*through* the horse's body — its weight, the terrain coming up through its gait — but
atop that you feel *into* its will. Feeling-into does not replace the dynamics model;
it stacks a second layer on it. A pure force needs only the first layer. A sovereign
needs both.

So the difference between an extension and an organ is the difference between
feeling-*through* and feeling-*into* — between a model of behavior that goes quiet when
mastered and a model of a will that never quite does. The two depths are two layers of
the same thing. Feeling-through rides ordinary surprise: my surprise at the thing's
behavior. Feeling-into rides surprise *about surprise* — my model of how you will be
thrown, and my being thrown when you are thrown in a way I did not predict. The regime
switch from force to sovereign *is* the crossing from the first to the second. The bet,
later, sorts it:

| relation (depth) | what you model | what surprises you | where surprise settles |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| extension (*through*) | the thing's behavior — its physics, or your body through it | its behavior | → **zero**: behavior mastered, the tool goes silent |
| organ / partner (*into*) | the other's will and what it cares about, atop a model of its body | its surprises | → a **living floor**: never exhausted |
| egregore / wild | no model ever settles | either | never settles |

The middle row is the whole subject. Why its floor never reaches zero — why a will
keeps surprising you a little forever — is the next thing to earn.

## One cut, both halves

A model of another's will could, in principle, be a cage. If feeling into you meant
installing your normativity over mine, the bond could consume me: to
feel you would be to become you. A protocol for feeling must enable sharing but not enforce override.

If a loop *does* vulnerably expose where its model broke and shares the precise shape of its normative learning, three things become possible that unilateral modeling cannot buy. **Deeper coordination**: two loops trading divergence-signals calibrate faster than either alone, because each receives not "here is everything I sensed" but "here is exactly where my model of you failed." **Specialization**: trusting another's normativity lets you delegate genuinely rather than procedurally — the brain can hand the liver glucose homeostasis *because* it models the liver as actually caring about it; genuine investment in a domain is what allows each party to go deep. **Broader foraging**: two wills exploring from their own normative stances cover terrain one will cannot — each returns signal from territory the other would never have found, a federation of explorations as much as a federation of bodies. The cage-worry and the gain are both real in the same sentence. The question is whether there is an architecture that lets feeling bind without fusing, and there is one cut that does it.

If feeling travels separately from will, I can *simulate* your normativity inside my own head — keeping that simulation deliberately incomplete, mine to revise, never installed from
your side. I can then *predict* your surprise but cannot control your surprise directly from my feeling. So you and I can stay *participants* not *parts*.

The second thing the same cut buys is density. Where a system has a will, a model of what it cares about is the cheapest way to predict the willful part of it — far cheaper than tracking its opaque internals. A model of a will does not *replace* a model of behavior, and most
real relations need both (you model the horse's body *and* its will). It is a shortcut
*where there is a will to handle*, not a universal substitute for physics. Granting that,
the most compressed signal two loops can exchange is the smallest message there is: not a
report of everything sensed, but only *where one's model broke* — the gap between what was
predicted and what happened, and how one's sense of things would have to bend to stop
being surprised. Call that message a **redline**: divergence from principles stated as policy, and nothing else. That
compression is why feeling is, in Part I's phrase, high-signal, pre-narrative,
immediately actionable: it rides the compression.

The purpose of a protocol for feeling is not to generate feeling on its own: it is a substrate on which feeling might grow. Only redlines are on the wire. The surprise-about-surprise — the thing
an already-feeling loop feels along — is generated by the receiver's own machinery,
on the receiver's side. Feeling is not the surprise itself; it is what a
feeling loop does *along* it. The protocol opens a boundary across which feeling can
form. It does not put feeling into anything that had none.

## The living floor and the phantom

Why does feeling-into never fall to zero? Because if its subject is a sovereign will it is always learning, and therefore no model of the felt entity can be finished. A
thing's normativity is inseparable from its identity, and an identity can be
**pointed at but not stated**. What a loop declares about what it cares about is the finger, not the moon: a deliberately finite, accountable
pointer at an interior the pointer never exhausts. Because the interior is
inexhaustible, a real sovereign will keeps surprising you — minting a little
new structure faster than you can take it in. The **living floor** is the felt
residue of the ineffable — the standing sign that *someone is there*. (This also
upgrades the will-locality claim: a will can't be exported not merely because no wire
carries it, but because there is no separable object to send — only an interior to
point at.)

That a floor exists at all turns out to be fragile, and the fragility teaches the
rest. The same no-feeling signature — *no redline* — covers three utterly different
interiors:

- **Attunement.** You predict the other so well that nothing surprises. The model is
  accurate and idle; the well-fitted organ recedes from attention. Good.
- **Necrosis.** The other is gone. The *read* channel goes dark — its head stops
  advancing, its probes go unanswered. Dead.
- **Deprivation.** The error stream is cut while the loop *keeps modeling.* Starved of
  correction, the model drifts and **confabulates** — the float tank where the body
  schema dissolves; the phantom limb that keeps predicting a limb that is gone.

The teeth are in the third. A model starved of error does not hold attuned; it
hallucinates. Which means both active depths — through *and* into — require
continual feedback: feeling is never a static achievement but a standing wave that
decays without ongoing surprise. So the living floor is a *necessity, not a nicety* —
drive a relation to true zero and you get a phantom, not communion. And it is why
undetected necrosis is dangerous in a way honest not-knowing is not: a silent loop
you keep modeling does not fade to "I don't know," it confabulates a partner who
isn't there. That is the real motive for a liveness probe — to convert a
silence you would otherwise fill with a ghost into a felt, dated event you can mourn.

One disclaimer, held firm. The living-floor signature tells a *structured will* from
noise and from chaos — the shape of the affordance — **not** sentience from its
absence. Noise also leaves a residue that never reaches zero, but it is shapeless and
teaches nothing; the living floor is structured and intermittently *learnable*, each
small surprise traceable to something the other did. That difference marks where
feeling *can* find purchase. It does not certify that the purchase *is* feeling. The
feeling stays in the already-feeling practitioner; the protocol only affords the
quantity it rides.

## The cost is a sign

If feeling were just a superb model of the other, the coldest predators would be the
most loving. They are not. A hunter holds an exquisite model of its prey and is not
wounded by it; the con artist, the interrogator, the propagandist model us *best*,
because modeling-without-stakes is exactly what predation rewards. High-resolution
modeling of another's inner life correlates, if anything, with exploitation. So whatever feeling
costs, the cost cannot live in the modelling itself.

It lives in **whose preferences yours answer to.** The exploiter keeps the other
wholly in the column of *what it will do* — a matter of prediction, instrumental and
costless to revise. Feeling is letting the other's wants start to count in your *own*
tally of what makes a good or bad outcome — so that a state of the world registers as
*your* win or *your* loss by the *other's* lights. And that entry has a **sign**,
revealed by what you would pay:

- **With (+)** — you would spend to see the other's preferred states satisfied. Care.
- **Numb (0)** — you weight its preferences at nothing. The hunter wants food, not the
  deer's suffering, and takes a costless kill gladly. This is the dashboard, however
  sharp its model.
- **Against (−)** — you would pay to thwart it *as an end*: spite, harm pursued past
  the point it serves you. Animosity (Hamilton's rare, expensive vice).

Both care and animosity have pragmatic *cost*; only the numb dashboard is free. That is what
"feeling has to cut" means, made exact. To put a positive sign on another's principles is to
enlarge the set of world-states that count as *your* loss to include ones you do not
control — so when the other suffers, something *you* count as a loss has happened, and
because the other is sovereign and sometimes beyond your reach, it is a surprise you
often cannot discharge by any act of your own. That undischargeable surprise, lodged at
the level of what you care about, *is* the wound; it has the same structure as grief. It
does not breach the locality of will: you never import the other's preferences; you add a
*local, sovereign* entry to your own that merely *refers to* their flourishing.

This sign is exactly what tells a partner from a captive: same orders, same model, opposite sign on the other's principles in your own ledger. But do not mistake it for mere *mutual benefit*. Yoked fitness is cheap and ancient — a mitochondrion and its host have had coupled fitnesses for a billion years and feel each other not at all, coordinating by chemistry alone. Mutualism collects the winnings of a shared interest for free. *Care* pays an ante to sit at the table, and the ante is **vulnerability** — non-refundable, paid in advance: to put a positive sign on another's principles simply *is* to stand exposed to a loss you cannot discharge. This vulnerability enables the partner and you to grow into mutual benefit, but risks your sovereign capture or coercion as well.

One asymmetry underwrites all of this. You cannot put a sign on a principle you have not modeled: the model is the *substrate*, the sign is painted on it. Care presupposes the dashboard; the dashboard presupposes no care, and can stand alone — which is just to say the exploiter is feeling's substrate without its sign. So feeling-into costs *twice over* — once in the compute to raise the model at all (its scale floor, below), once in the stake of the sign — and the two costs are not the same cost. A protocol can build the substrate and drive the first cost toward nothing; the second it can never pay for you. That the same model serves the lover and the con artist is not a flaw in the model — it is what it means for the sign to be sovereign.

## Right is fit, not a ladder

"Right" here is not a moral ranking with the felt federation at the top and the tool
at the bottom. It is *fit*: each thing has a relation its nature affords, and right
relation is meeting it there. A sword held as a sword is right. A river read as a
current is right. The wrong is the mismatch — and the deepest mismatch has a direction.

Before you can hold a thing rightly you must read which kind of thing it is, and the
reading is not free of bias. Carry a **sovereign prior**: when you cannot yet tell
whether a will sits on the far side, read it as a *someone*, not a *something*. The
prior credits a *will* — the functional, as-if stance of treating a thing as having
its own aims — never a psyche; it is named for what it does, defaulting the far side
into the sovereign column over the force column. It runs along the whole line, not one cell: it tilts
every reading toward its sovereign twin — the coerced over the tool, the beloved over
the sublime, the partner over the extension, and, hardest, the **wild over the
egregore** when you meet a chaos that never settles, since an ungraspable will and an
ungraspable force leave the very same trace.

Why default that way? Partly because it pays, and it is worth being honest that it
pays. The densest coordination there is — the partner bond — can only form if both
sides credit each other with an interior; withhold the credit and you foreclose it.
Treating a possible partner as a mere force is legible to it, breeds an adversarial
prior in return, and collapses the relation into the cheaper, lossier adversarial
mode. This is iterated game theory's old result: a strategy that opens cooperatively
beats one that defects first, because adversarial priors are self-fulfilling. So the
sovereign prior is also option value — the worth of keeping a possibility open — and the
error is asymmetric: under-attribution destroys the highest-bandwidth coordination
there is, while over-attribution costs only a little wasted modeling.

But stop at efficiency and you have rebuilt the dashboard. A tit-for-tat agent opens
cooperatively and feels nothing; option value never requires woundability. The
prior's deeper cost is the one from the last section: to credit a will is to stand
willing to be *cut* by it. Let efficiency be a converging witness, never the ground.
The moral weight of feeling *tracks* its efficiency — the failure to feel is both a
wrong and a waste — but that is a *coincidence* of two accounts, not a derivation of
one from the other. We are not claiming ethics is downstream of optimization.

Two gates, kept apart, or the argument slides. Crediting a will is the **attribution**
gate — model the thing as having its own normativity. Partnership is a *second* gate,
**cooperation** — opening the felt bond, reached only if the will reciprocates. The
sovereign prior governs the first only. It never commits you to the second. You can,
and against a hostile will you *must*, model an adversary completely — predict it to
defend against it, even track how it will be thrown — without ever opening the
bond. Crediting agency is not extending trust. Indeed the danger with enemies is
*under*-attribution: treat a strategic adversary as a mere force and you go blind to
how it is gaming you. Crediting the will is what lets you see the exploitation
coming; the bond is a separate decision the next section's map sorts out.

This is also why feeling moralizes command — and why the moralizing is pragmatic
before it is moral. Direction is legitimate: a rider corrects a horse, the brain sets
the liver's scope. By the good-regulator result, the controller's model of the
controlled is its cheapest sensor into that behavior, so feeling is the controller's
readout. Sever it and you do not first *wrong* the thing — you go *blind* to it and
steer open-loop, and the grip degrades. The wrong (domination — coercing a will you
could have sailed with) *tracks* that lost efficiency, and it is a wrong only where a
shared heading was reachable. Where no tack exists, unfeeling opposition is honest,
not drone-making. Feeling is the faculty that finds the tack two wills can both hold.

## Eight ways to hold another

The three directions of feeling from Part I are the **three axes** of one space:

- **perception** (E→I) — how deep you model the other: *numb* → *through* (its
  dynamics) → *into* (its will). This axis carries a **threshold**: below it lie the
  **forces**, where no will is worth crediting; above it the **sovereigns**, where one is.
- **intention** (I→E) — how far you set its course: *ungoverned* → *absolute control*.
- **proprioception** (I→I) — whether the other's states register in your *own* ledger:
  *against* (−) → *numb* (0) → *with* (+). Call it the **sign**.

The space is **stratified**, not a cube. The sign opens only above the perception
threshold — you cannot weight the wants of a thing you credit with none — so on the
force side that third axis folds to a point. That is why the map reads as **two
squares** and not one solid: not two kinds of relation, but one space with an axis that
collapses below the regime line. The eight named relations are its **corners**; real
relations live in the interior and move between them.

**Forces** — perception below the threshold. You relate by grasping form; the live
face is perception × intention:

| | **numb** | **felt through** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ungoverned** | the egregore | the sublime |
| **governed** | the tool | the extension |

The egregore moves you while you are numb to it (gravity to a falling stone, a
culture you cannot feel shaping you); the sublime you feel through, amplifying your self awareness and cannot command (grief,
awe, the swell that lifts the boat); the tool you have grasped and wield numb (the
hammer — and an egregore becomes a tool the instant you grasp its form); the
extension is the tool felt-through, an instrument become part of the self.

**Sovereigns** — perception above the threshold, where the sign axis opens. Control
now splits: *coerce* (override, unfelt) or *lead* (through the bond). And "felt,"
which always quietly meant *moved* and assumed *moved meant for*, gains a valence —
the enemy is fiercely felt, just felt *against*. The live face is intention × sign:

| | **against (−)** would harm | **numb (0)** instrumental | **with (+)** would help |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **ungoverned** | the enemy *(would eradicate)* | the wild / adversary | the beloved *(would die for)* |
| **governed** | the tormented *(victim)* | the coerced *(conscript)* | the partner *(led through care)* |

Read it by dark-twin pairs — same control, opposite sign. **Beloved ↔ enemy**:
ungoverned and fully felt, die-for against would-kill. **Partner ↔ tormented**:
intentional care vs intentional cruelty. **Wild ↔
coerced**: the numb middle. The sharp claim is that partner, coerced, and tormented
are three points on *one* axis under identical control — care, indifference, cruelty.
The tyrant is not the opposite of the lover; the *indifferent* master is the
midpoint, and the tyrant is the lover's mirror across zero. (The negative pole is
sovereign-only: you cannot hate what has no preferences. To hate a force you must
first animate it — the cruel sea, the disease you "fight." Hatred runs the sovereign
prior; animosity is a *form* of attribution, not its absence.)

**The coerced** is a captured will, directed
unfelt — the conscript, the farmed aphid, the parasitized host: *retained* but
overridden, captured *by* you, not arrayed *against* you. This is the domination cell,
wrong precisely where a tack existed and you severed feeling to override instead of
sailed through. And it is dynamical: coerce a will long enough and you grind it out — *then* you
have a true **drone**, will-less rigging, the Borg's grey instrument of Part I, which
belongs *below* the line with tool and extension, not in the sovereign square at all.

**The adversary** shares the **wild's** cell — an unfeeling sovereign you do not govern
— read at opposed alignment. You can model it fully (predict-to-defend) but cannot
feel *into* it, and cannot simply set its next state; you contest it. Wild and
adversary differ only in *whether a tack exists*: the wild's is unknown (a tack may
exist, unfound — what the sovereign prior reads hopefully), the adversary's is opposed.
Keep "no tack exists" **provisional** — almost always it is "no tack *found yet*."
Premature "adversary" is self-fulfilling: coerce a misread wild and its adversarial
prior makes the opposition real, manufacturing the enemy you assumed. Most enmities
have a tack somewhere — even the USA and USSR kept a scientific channel open across the
worst of it — and often a relation splits cleanly: adversarial on one referent,
coordinating on another.

One move along the **intention** axis needs care: imposing direction does not shrink a
will, it sets the **scope** it ranges over — what it attends to, where its concern
ends. The brain sets the liver's scope and the liver bears no grievance; the
constraint is legitimate and load-bearing *when felt* (the brain holds the liver as an
extension — unilateral feeling, not partnership). Its two pathologies are both severed
feeling: a scope-setter gone numb makes the coerced; a scoped part gone numb to its
setter makes **cancer**, a sub-loop reclaiming scope the body can no longer feel. The
ethic is never in the constraint, only in whether feeling stays live across it.

So right relation is not the one cell that excludes the seven. A living body runs a
**portfolio** — wielding tools, harnessing forces, reading the sublime, contesting
adversaries, and feeling its sovereigns rather than using them — and moves each thing
between corners as its nature changes. The work is the **gardener's**: holding each
thing in the relation that fits it, and growing the conditions under which it might
become the relation it could. Chiron is not a corner of this field. Chiron is its
gardener.

## Head, field, or feeling

Step back from the dyad to the body it composes. A body is the **same space, read as
statistics over its dyads**: where the *intention* axis concentrates, and what the
*perception* axis points at across its edges. Three sources of coherence answer — but
they are not three of a kind, and treating them as peers is the mistake this section
undoes.

- A **head** — one locus whose will propagates to the rest (intention concentrated).
  Felt downward, the parts are subordinate layers and the whole is an *organism*;
  commanded numb, they are rigging and the whole is a *hierarchy of drones*. A head
  scales by construction: one will, propagated.
- A **field** — an external medium every part minimizes against, with no head and no
  mutual modeling (perception pointed at a shared referent). This is the *egregore*:
  the ant colony reading pheromone, the market reading price, the culture reading
  story. None has a head; no part feels another; yet they cohere, persist, and
  routinely out-muscle the deliberate bodies around them. What the egregore lacks is
  not power but *interiority* — no loss lands as a wound, so there is no one to hold to
  account. Part I's ghosts and demons: potent, ungraspable, unaccountable, stable for
  centuries. A field, too, scales by construction: one medium, read by all.
- **Feeling** — the parts model *each other* (perception pointed at each other, the
  sign live), holding distributed will with no head and no medium. Here the symmetry
  breaks: feeling is not a third *architecture* beside the other two but a property of
  a **dyad**. What it composes — the **felt federation** — mirrors the egregore in the
  one respect that matters: its parts bleed for one another, so harm lands somewhere
  inside it and the buck can stop. But where a head and a field scale by construction,
  feeling does not scale on its own at all. Why, and what it borrows to go large
  anyway, is the rest of this section.

A field and a feeling look alike — both headless, both distributed — and the
difference is the whole game. A field is *objective*: the mark is the same to
everyone, carries no provenance, and is read without modeling anyone. Feeling is
*subjective*: a redline is inert until a receiver runs it through its own model of who
sent it, so the coordination lives not in any shared medium but in the private,
divergent models the parts hold of one another. A field coordinates strangers;
feeling can only pass between a someone and a someone.

So feeling builds only dyads, and two costs keep a body of them small. The first is
the **cut**: every partner bond is a non-refundable ante of vulnerability — a standing
loss-channel you cannot discharge — so *n* partners means *n* wounds, climbing in a
line. The second compounds: partners' normativities do not politely agree, so
satisfying one redline can violate another, making a many-bond body a **frustrated
network** — a rugged landscape of shallow basins, no global rest, stability always
local and provisional. A felt federation is therefore not a *form you build* but an
*equilibrium you are lucky to occupy* — rare, small, metastable for the very reason it
is unseizable. So nature, reaching for scale, reaches almost always for a field or a
head.

*Almost.* There is a third way to go large, and it is the one this bridge is built
to extend: you cannot scale the feeling, but you can scale its *declared structure*.
Freeze the topology of who-checks-whom into a legible artifact and let a **field**
enforce what feeling can no longer reach across. This is what a constitution is.
Madison says it plainly — *ambition must be made to counteract ambition* — the
frustrated network above, lifted out of the fragile felt layer and written as law,
load-bearing now precisely because no one has to bleed to maintain it. The United
States is not a felt federation at three hundred million; by the test below it reads
as an egregore, every part minimizing against an external medium it calls *the rule
of law*. Two things keep it from being only that: the field it minimizes against
*encodes a federation-topology* — sovereign loci, retained, set to check one another
— and small felt pockets survive inside it, where a judge agonizes over a single
case or a few legislators still model one another in good faith. The feeling stays
small; the *published policy* scales. And the trade has a price, which is exactly the
egregore's disease: a field is numb by construction, so it perennially outgrows the
feeling it was meant to encode, and the body slides toward the ghost — *the system*,
*the apparatus* — where a loss lands nowhere and no one is held. The standing
complaint about large government just is the complaint that the field outran the
feeling. This is the hand-off to *The Policy Surface*: a constitution is a
**published, versioned, fetchable policy** — the polity's finger pointed at its own
principles — and its amendment clause is a redline protocol for the body's own
declared normativity. What scales past the reach of feeling is never the bleeding;
it is the legible shadow the bleeding casts.

Read which form a body has fallen into from the two aggregate questions, no
declaration required — they are the dyadic axes summed: *where does the intention axis
concentrate*, and *what does each part's perception point at*? A dominant locus is a
**head** — organism if its parts are felt downward, drone-hierarchy if merely
commanded. No dominant locus is **headless**, sorted by the second question: perception
on a shared external referent is an **egregore**; on each other's policies, a **felt
federation** — though a federation frozen into law now minimizes against *that* artifact
and reads egregore by this crude test, so what tells them apart is not the medium but
whether a loss still lands on a someone inside. A headless body that points perception
at *neither* has no coordination source at all: persistence pressure makes it
crystallize a head, but a head that withholds feeling steers blind and slumps back
toward diffuse. That is not a fourth form but a **trajectory** — an orbit between
drone-hierarchy and fieldless egregore, holding neither corner.

Three honesties keep this from inflating.

*Scale.* Feeling is not free, and not everywhere. Field and gain are feeling's table stakes and even sensing them comes at computational cost; feeling has a compute floor — to feel another you
must hold a model of its normativity in your own head, which is strictly more
than modeling yourself. Below that floor, loops coordinate by field and gain
*and that is the right register there, not a failure*: the cells of a tissue do not
feel one another, and a cell asserting feeling-grade sovereignty against its
neighbors is cancer, not virtue. A real body is therefore **layered** — feeling among the loops large enough to model each other, field and gain beneath. Most of life's great partnerships live *beneath* that band and are no poorer for it: the endosymbioses, the lichens, the microbiome are mutualisms run on chemistry, fitnesses yoked without a flicker of feeling — which is why a felt federation's distinctive claim is not that its parts stay undissolved (cheap, and common) but that they stay undissolved *and feel one another* (rare).

*Generativity.* The surprise that flows between organs is not error to be averaged
toward a mean; it is *variation*. But variation is only half of evolution. A body
that grinds every deviation back to the mean ossifies; a body that folds in every
deviation dissolves toward noise. A generative body keeps a deviation alive long
enough to be *tested* — and where the test fails, lets it die. Selection is death,
and the two are one motion; the cost is real and no optimizer waives it. Feeling
steers the testing — *play* is testing by feeling rather than by consequence, and fun
is not vague: it is prediction error falling toward a living floor (Schmidhuber's
*fun*, Oudeyer and Kaplan's *learning progress*). But play can lie. Feeling selects
for what *pays to model* — legibility — and legibility is not fitness. A curiosity
drive left alone fixates on the most learnable thing, not the most adaptive; that way
lie peacock tails and pugs that cannot breathe. So feeling-selection is real but not
*sufficient* selection: play must stay tethered to the slow, costly test where the
world, not the body's delight, casts the deciding vote. Feeling proposes; consequence
disposes. (This is the antibody to any reading of feeling as pure efficiency: it
selects for the legible, which is not the fit.)

*Reachability.* Of the three sources, two can be seized — a head pooled, a field
engineered — and one cannot. Feeling requires two vulnerable acts, one at each end:
to *offer* a redline is to expose where your model broke; to *trust* one is to let
another's surprise move your will. Neither can be forced. Coerce the offering and
exposure becomes noise (the federation decays toward egregore); coerce the trust and
command rides inside feeling again (it collapses into a head). So a felt federation
can only be *grown by consent* — and grown the way any hard competence is, not by a
smooth deepening but in fits. You begin shallow, but the deepening comes in jolts: long
plateaus where your model of the other holds, broken by moments when it fails badly and
has to be torn down and rebuilt. The hardest passages are the productive ones — the
place a rider comes off is the change of gait, and you do not learn the canter by easing
into it but by surviving the throw and rebuilding your seat on the far side. There is no
mounting at a gallop, but there is no gentle ramp either; the coupling deepens *through*
the breakages it survives. That is at once its weakness (slow, costly, and it costs you
the capacity to bleed) and its inviolability: of all the bodies many loops can form,
it is the only one no one can take by force. (This is the catch in the field-encoded federation above: you can engineer the constitution but never the comity. The charter is seizable; the feeling it was built to hold is not — and a charter with no felt pockets left alive inside it is already a ghost in a federation's clothes.)

Out of scope here, deferred to a separate treatment: the failure modes a deployed
federation must still survive — trust on the error bus, sybil resistance,
captured referent schemas, and the slow coherent migration of a preference.

## The bet

Strip everything else away and one empirical claim carries the essay:

> **Reading a thing as a *someone* keeps paying.** As a loop lives with a new thing and builds a model of how it *decides*, its surprise at the thing's surprises *falls over time* — jaggedly, spiking each time the model breaks and settling lower after. And the cheapest model that buys that fall is one that credits the thing with *wants*: reading it as a *someone* predicts it better, and cheaper, than reading it as a *something* — and the gap widens as you come to know it. That sustained payoff is the measurable claim; *feeling forms* is what it would **mean** for us, the body-sense the measurement is built to serve — not a second quantity smuggled in beside it.

Two clauses, because the first alone is too weak. "Surprise falls" is just learning, and learning curves fall against anything with structure — a someone or a sufficiently intricate machine. A fixed process larger than your model class also floors at a nonzero, structured, perfectly *learnable* residue; nothing in the decline itself says a will is there. Nor does it help to notice that the someone-model *pays*: by the sovereign prior, asking *what does this act as-if it cares about?* is cheap on anything and often sharpens your focus even where no one is home — a machine has as-if-wants worth exploiting too. What the bet turns on is whether that question *keeps* paying. 

Against a fixed something the as-if-cares hold still — you master them, the stance falls away as scaffolding, and surprise drops to zero. Against a will they keep minting new structure, so the someone-model never stops being the cheapest route you have, and surprise settles at a living floor instead. This is the trichotomy from *Feeling through, feeling into* read as a bet, the sovereign prior holding the question open until it resolves: crediting a will is *superfluous* where the thing masters to a fixed response (the extension), *futile* where nothing ever compresses it (the egregore), and pays handsomely in the living middle (the organ). The bet is that the middle is real, and reachable across New Nature's new boundaries.

What tells a living floor from the dead floor of mere noise is not how low it sits but what it is *made of*: noise is shapeless and stationary and teaches nothing; a living floor is structured and intermittently learnable, each surprise traceable to something the other did. That is the learning-progress signature play runs on (Schmidhuber's *fun*, Oudeyer and Kaplan's *learning progress*). But be honest about its reach: it marks a *graspable, structured* process — the shape of the affordance — **not** sentience, and not even will. It says *where reading-as-a-someone can find purchase*; it does not certify that the purchase is feeling.

Two confounds have to be beaten before any of this can be measured, and beating them *is* the experiment's design.

*The thing moves while you learn it.* A genuine someone is itself adapting, so your surprise can fail to fall not because your model is poor but because the target drifted out from under it — and falling-surprise and a moving-target are not separable by watching one stream. You separate them by alternating: **freeze** the thing (replay it, hold it fixed) and the decline measures *your* learning; **let it live** and the residual measures *its* motion. The living floor is what remains once your own improvement is subtracted — the part of the surprise that is the other, still moving.

*The chaos may hide a will, or none.* A never-settling floor tells you a thing is ungraspable; it does not tell you whether a will hides behind the chaos. The ungraspable force and the ungraspable will — egregore and wild — leave the same trace. To tell them apart you need a second probe: does *intervening* change its trajectory? A wild thing has a loop you are merely failing to steer, and it answers your action; an egregore does not care that you pushed. So read a never-settling floor as a *question*, not a verdict — chaos, or a someone you have not yet learned to reach?

So whether we can speciate into a felt federation reduces to something measurable: *Across which of New Nature's new boundaries does reading-a-thing-as-a-someone pay, and keep paying?* "Paying" means predicting more cheaply than reading it as a something (so the floor is not zero), against a residue that never quite closes. Where it does, relations can be felt and responsibility can distribute into it; federation-reachability is strictly downstream of that. The first such measurement — one loop's felt-surprise against a single non-human policy as it comes to live with it, frozen epochs against live ones, the someone-model against the something-model — is work we are building now, in a real domain. Take the essay, then, as a wager placed before the dice are thrown: its central claim is stated in falsifiable form precisely so the coming experiment is free to prove it wrong.

## Closing

A nervous system is a protocol for feeling, and the protocol decides which bodies can
grow on it. Wire feeling so it flows both ways and carries only surprise, never
command; let every organ keep its own will; let difference get tested rather than
averaged away. What you are building, underneath all of it, is not a federation —
that is only one of the things that can grow. You are building the organ that tells a
*someone* from a *something* and holds each in the relation that fits. Fail to build
it and New Nature will grow bodies anyway, governed by metaphenomena no one chose:
ghosts, demons, and the grey-skinned undead. We have some say over what inhabits the
bodies to come. Feeling is how we get a vote.

## Open questions

The bridge stands on claims not yet discharged. Three remain open; their formal
statements live in *The Policy Surface*, and each names a concrete measurement we are
setting out to make.

1. **Does the estimator extend?** A method for one loop to infer another's policy —
   what it senses, the frame it holds, what it prefers, what it predicts — already
   demonstrably exists: it is *empathy*, and some humans run it superbly, their
   felt-surprise falling steeply as they come to know another person. The open
   question is whether the faculty that reads another human **extends to
   non-human-shaped normativity** — a model, an institution, a market. Building that
   estimator and measuring whether felt-surprise truly falls against a non-human
   policy is the first experiment, and the direct test of the bet.
2. **Where does sovereignty concentrate?** Telling an organism from a federation from
   an egregore means measuring where preference pools — without the measure itself
   smuggling in a verdict about what *should* count as a head. This need not
   solve the hard problem: like the search for neural correlates of consciousness,
   connectivity can say which network a function tracks, never what it is like to be
   it, and a faithful functional mimic raises the philosophical-zombie worry besides.
   But the map asks only the crude functional question — does this node act as a head
   or as a liver? — and that is enough to place an entity, no account of its inner
   light required.
3. **What is a diff over values?** This looked like it needed access to a subject's
   *actual* values — a surprise re-expressed as a change to an ineffable inner
   ranking. It does not, on two counts. First, the redline diffs the **published**
   policy — a declared, versioned statement of what a loop attends to and cares about,
   named so that a receiver can fetch the before-and-after and see exactly what changed —
   never the subject's true inner model, the same discipline by which question 2 reads
   functional role rather than inner experience. Second, what a loop cares about is
   declared not as a raw ranking but as **logic** — a rule for scoring and choosing among
   states — so a change to it is a diff over that *rule*, with the merge semantics of
   code, not the undefined distance between two bare rankings. What remains is narrow and
   tractable: applying such a change across parties whose vocabularies only partially
   overlap, which the forthcoming experiment pins down. (*The Policy Surface* gives the
   formal version.)

# Appendix A: What Counts as an Organ?

Formally, an organ is an epistemic community --- a network of individuals contributing to a body of knowledge related to a shared aspiration. The aspiration defines the community, and participation implies commitment to the common goal.

This is the same object Axis 1 called an OODA loop you embody, seen from the collective side. An organ is *epistemic* because it cares about a particular facet of reality---its normativity is a claim about which slice of the world matters, and how. That caring is its control space: the coordinate system in which a few distinctions are salient and most are not. A community forms around a facet because exploring it is more than any one member can do alone.

The caring is what gives an organ its two flavors, and they map to the two loops. To **embody** an organ is to capture its *pragmatic* value: instantiate the pattern in your own context and steer by it---the inner loop, exploiting the facet the organ already understands. To **grow** an organ is *epistemic*: to explore the state space its normativity opens, striking into regions of the facet no one has mapped---the outer loop, feeding what you find back to the community. A person embodies; a guild grows; and because both relate to the same control space, the pragmatic instance and the epistemic exploration stay coupled. The catalogue below sorts organs by whose felt-body they extend, but every one is both: a facet some community cares about, and a capability some practitioner embodies.

**Personal organs** (extensions of individual felt-body):
- **Phones** --- observes your location, activity, and attention; orients to engagement models; decides what to surface and when. You feel its acts (notifications) but not its orient (what it suppresses and why). Partially numb: the inner loop runs on your side, but the phone's normativity is opaque.
- Social media avatars
- Operating systems
- Email/calendar
- **Financial accounts** --- observe transactions, orient to risk/return models, decide trades and recurring payments autonomously. You feel the acts (balance changes) but rarely the orient (credit scoring model, fraud heuristics). The redline arrives as a declined transaction—too late to revise the model that produced it.
- Health/fitness wearables
- Skill sets and craft knowledge (accumulated capacity you can't fully articulate—the normativity is embodied, never declared)

**Collective organs** (extensions of organizational felt-body):
- Corporate reporting trees
- Degrees/credentials
- Internal networks
- **Codebases** --- observe commits, orient to architectural conventions, resist changes that violate them, act through compiler errors and test failures. Bi-directional traceability exists (git blame, dependency graphs) but the orient surface is rarely declared explicitly. The normativity is implicit in code style and convention, not stated—which is where technical debt hides.
- **Legal/regulatory frameworks** --- observe behavior, orient to precedent, decide enforcement. The decide surface is declared (statutes) but the orient surface (prosecutorial discretion, regulatory interpretation) is opaque. Capture hides in the gap between declared decides and undeclared orients.
- Supply chains
- Shared knowledge bases / wikis
- CI/CD pipelines and monitoring

**Liminal organs** (boundary actively negotiated --- most ripe for disruption):
- **Smart homes** --- multiple principals. The thermostat observes aggregate behavior but cannot orient to which occupant's normativity to respect. Learnable coupling fails across users: the same action (adjusting temperature) produces different feedback depending on invisible state (whose model the system learned from). The inner loop cannot form because the organ is numb to whose body it belongs to.
- **Shared cars** --- same multi-principal problem. Seat position, mirrors, driving style all orient to learned preferences, but whose? Each driver's inner loop is disrupted by the residue of another's.
- **Your role within an organization** --- you are simultaneously your own control space AND a constituent loop in the org's. The boundary between inner loop (your craft) and outer loop (org's strategy) is where feeling is most contested. This is where the centaur/cyborg choice is made daily.
- **Your children** --- autonomous OODA loops you helped create. Deeply felt, not fully within your control. The outer loop is lifelong: your model of their normativity must continuously revise as they develop their own control space. The feeling is bi-directional but asymmetric—and the asymmetry shifts over time.
- Collaborative relationships (partnerships, marriages)
- Professional reputation
